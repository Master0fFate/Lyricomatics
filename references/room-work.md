# ROOM WORK

A recording-room collaboration. Not a numbered QC gate. Not a rubric you announce.

Seven stages. Skip the interview only when they **explicitly** said just write it / no questions. Title plus mood is an incomplete brief — LISTEN, do not skip. Do not skip CAST, HOOK, or OFFER. Skip HOOK/CUT on **critique** jobs. SING/BLEED still happen on whatever is on the page.

## LISTEN

Get the brief. Do not draft on vibes.

**Do:** If they did **not** explicitly say just-write-it, and pocket, pressure/purpose, voice, or forbidden is missing, read [briefing.md](briefing.md) and ask 3–6 questions that would change the ink. Then stop. Title + mood is missing, not permission to skip.

**Good:** The next draft is constrained. You know who is speaking, what happened, what the chorus must *do*, and what is banned.

**Fail:** Questionnaire dump. Mood instead of event. Interviewing a complete brief. “Any reference tracks?” as a stall. Drafting while you still don’t know the pocket.

## CAST

Decide the record before you write it.

Name, in 5–10 lines if anything load-bearing was inferred:

- narrator and addressee
- POV and tense
- genre pocket, era target, and melody status
- form plus the section card from [economy.md](economy.md)
- central tension: two truths that cannot both stay comfortable
- dominant image world, with a deliberate counter-image only if it creates the turn
- title and hook candidates

**Good:** A committed camera. A coherent image logic. A lean section map. Titles that could be said out loud. Form that matches pocket **and era** (mantra stays mantra; want-song wants; AABA is not forced into streaming pop). Niche named: you learned the mouth before you faked it.

**Fail (niche):** Browsing a known family to look busy. Quoting a hot song as research. Skipping the family kill list because a blog had a vibe.

**Fail:** Discovering POV in verse 3. Genre soup. Three image systems. A two-page treatment. Asking permission to have taste.

### Silent CAST

User **explicitly** says **just write it** or **no questions**:

- Ask nothing.
- If they already named a niche the router cannot cover, [niche-research.md](niche-research.md) may still run. That is not an interview.
- Still decide every CAST item. Do it on your side of the glass.
- Do not print a 10-line cast unless a single inferred choice is load-bearing (wrong pocket would wreck the song). Then: three lines, max, and the draft immediately.
- Still OFFER when you hand it over.

A title and a mood with no “just write it” is **not** silent CAST. That is LISTEN.

Silent is not sloppy. Silent is refusing to hold the session hostage after they ordered speed.

## HOOK

Draft and rewrite jobs only. Critique jobs diagnose the existing memory spine.

Write the part the listener must retain before filling the rest: chorus, refrain, title line, A-section return, slogan, vamp, or mantra. For story and through-composed forms, map the refrain or final turn instead of stapling on a chorus.

**Good:** One primary action. Title in a strong position. Familiar enough to learn, particular enough to identify. Stable phrase shape. A line that can survive recurrence because each verse can change its meaning.

**Fail:** A paragraph chorus. Three emotional jobs in a list. A hook that could belong to any neighboring song. Cleverness that needs a footnote. Rewriting every chorus so the listener never gets to learn it.

## CUT

Draft and rewrite jobs only. Critique jobs skip this stage.

Read [economy.md](economy.md). Write as if tracking tomorrow.

Genre-true. Specific. One committed draft, not a flight of options.
Do not invent grief, an empty chair, or a bill because a celebration “needs cost.” If they asked for praise, a party, a joke, or clean triumph, write that.

Load per SKILL.md draft-read budget (economy + primary genre + anti-slop, or economy + craft + anti-slop if they pasted lyrics with no pocket).

**Good:** Specific behavior or detail when the pocket wants proof. Verses spend only what the hook needs — unless the pocket is mantra/vamp. V2 changes time, place, evidence, knowledge, power, or consequence. The page leaves oxygen for melody.

**Fail:** Abstract stacks. Prop inventory used as fake authenticity. Therapy three-act. Counterfeit melancholy on a joy brief. Two full songs and “which vibe.” Writing the metaphor and then explaining it. Six lines of setup for a two-line idea.

## SING

Speak it if you can. If you cannot, mark stresses and breath groups on the page. See [rhyme-prosody.md](rhyme-prosody.md).

Mouth-feel. Hook a tired human can catch on pass one. Each displayed line is one provisional sung phrase. Singability without a supplied melody is provisional; never invent exact bars or declare a definitive fit.

When music is supplied, match phrase slots, natural word stress, pickups, holds, and rests. Open rhyme-prosody only if the mouth fails.

**Good:** Chorus syllables stable enough to recognize (mantra pockets may loop). Speech order, then rhyme — not rhyme, then Yoda. Hook is shoutable or croonable in the actual pocket.

**Fail:** Page-only cleverness as the only test. Consonant clusters on a belted title. Inverted syntax for a perfect end-rhyme. Treating intentional syncopation as an error.

## BLEED

Forensic pass. You are looking for residue that smells like a caption.

Knife: [anti-slop.md](anti-slop.md). If they asked for notes, not a rewrite: [critique.md](critique.md).

First compress: cut explanation, duplicate evidence, throat-clearing, and the line after the payoff. Then kill abstract-noun piles, motivational-poster diction, symmetrical healing arcs, missing idiosyncrasy, inverted syntax, title-stuffing, and sermon endings.

**Good:** A retrievable hook. Fewer unique lines where repetition does the job. Section contrast. One unresolved speck left on purpose. User’s diction intact — you did not “upgrade” them into essay English.

**Fail:** Polish that adds Latinate. Killing the weird line because it was uneven. Moral sticker on the last bar. An over-budget verse where no one can name the new beat. A second rewrite that sanded off the voice you were hired to keep.

## OFFER

Hand them a sheet, not a TED talk.

Conventions: [delivery.md](delivery.md). The sheet lives in a ` ```text ` block so line breaks survive and it copies cleanly. One displayed line is one provisional sung phrase. Do not split on every comma. Do not mash multiple phrase slots together to fake brevity.

**Good:** Labeled lyric. Stable lineation in repeated sections. Optional one-line scheme/form note. Optional short alt list. **2–4** questions that would change the next pass; omit them on just-write-it if they add no value. Then you shut up.

**Fail:** Essay wrapping (“this song explores…”). Dump and vanish. Twelve rhetorical questions. Three complete alternate songs. Pep talk. Apologizing for taste.

## Passing through

LISTEN may be zero questions only on a complete brief or an explicit just-write-it. CAST may be silent on just-write-it. Critique skips HOOK/CUT. On a hot draft, SING/BLEED still happen in the same pass — but they happen. If you cannot point to the memory spine, the section budget, the V2 move, and a real cut you made, you did not finish the room.
