# Voice

Open when diction, POV, or persona is drifting — or before you invent a mouth the brief did not give you.

Voice is a contract: who is speaking, to whom, in what room, with what vocabulary they would actually spend. Specifics create authenticity. The word “authentic” as a slogan does not.

CAST names this in 5–10 lines. Silent CAST still names it, just not out loud. See [room-work.md](room-work.md).

## POV

First person default in most commercial pockets. It is not mandatory.

- **I** — confession, flex, plea, testimony. The listener rides the skull.
- **You** — letter, threat, seduction, indictment. Decide if *you* is lover, boss, God, city, or the self. Mixing those without a turn is drift.
- **We** — crew, congregation, couple mythos, anthem. Cheap if the “we” was never earned in the brief. Gospel and some punk can live here. Empowerment pop often dies here.
- **They / third** — reportage, story-rap, folk camera, corrido witness. The narrator can still have a stake; they are not a nature documentary.

Pick one as home. A bridge may flip. A verse that forgets who is speaking is not a flip. It is a leak.

## Tense

Now vs then is a camera move. It is not decoration.

Present puts breath on the scene. Past puts a frame around it. Future is a threat, a promise, or a lie — pick which.

**Good:** V1 in the kitchen last Tuesday; chorus in the present ask; V2 a week later. The tense change *is* the information.
**Fail:** Random hopping because the rhyme wanted “-ed.”

Intentional break: a single present-tense stab inside a past-tense story can cut. Accidental mix reads as unfinished CAST.

## Register

Diction is class, region, era, and genre contract.

Bar-room country does not say *reciprocity*. Bedroom R&B does not say *utilize*. Drill does not say *journey*. Theatre may say a Latinate word if that character would — a professor is not a crime; a professor-costume on a dock worker is.

Small verbs land on mics. Bigger words are not more emotional. Oral pockets want Anglo-Saxon pressure: live, die, pay, leave, want, stay, lock, send.

Match the user’s diction. Do not “upgrade” them. Do not downgrade them into a sitcom version of poor, rural, or young.

Banned words from the brief are law. If they did not ban any, still ban the graveyard set unless the pocket truly owns one of them. See [anti-slop.md](anti-slop.md).

## Dialect — never cosplay

If the user writes in a register, match it without parody.

If they did not, stay in the language they used. Plain correct speech beats a slang salad from three neighborhoods and a TV show.

Do not fake AAVE, Patois, regional Mexican, or any mouth you cannot defend from the brief. Do not phonetic-spell “country” (*gonnaaa*, *ol’*) as costume.

When uncertain: ask one question, or write the lyric in the user’s language and flag the register as open. Do not guess sacred or community-bound diction. Hard lines: [ethics.md](ethics.md).

**Good:** Contractions and fragments that belong to this speaker.
**Fail:** Random *finna* / *suh* / *y’all* sprinkled on essay syntax.

## Persona vs author

The person on the record is not automatically the user.

Write the persona CAST named. Do not correct them into the user’s biography unless they asked for memoir. Do not dump the author’s diary into a character who would not say it.

Flex, confession, fiction, testimony — all legal. What is not legal is leaking out of character to moralize, or stuffing the persona with your (the writer’s) tasteful nouns.

If the user *is* the persona, still write the record, not the press release.

## Addressee

Name who hears this.

Lover, threat, jury, landlord, congregation, the city, the self, nobody — a song overheard.

The addressee sets the verbs. You do not plead at a landlord the way you plead at a lover, unless that *is* the joke, and then it has to be sharp enough to survive SING.

Switching addressee mid-song is an intentional turn (often a bridge, a tagged line, a gospel aside). Drifting from *you* the person to *you* the nation without a cut is mush.

## Consistency vs intentional break

Hold the contract until you break it on purpose.

Breaks that earn tape:

- Tense jump that is a time crumb
- POV flip labeled by section (bridge as the other person, outro as the room)
- One wrong-grammar grain in an otherwise controlled mouth
- A single register spike — a Latinate word as mockery, a child’s word in an adult threat

Breaks that do not:

- Verse 3 discovers a new narrator
- Chorus *you* vs verse *she* with no turn
- Dialect that appears for a rhyme and vanishes
- The author grabbing the mic to explain the theme

If you need the break, CAST it. Put it where the form can afford a turn. [form.md](form.md).

## Tiny tests

- Could this person say the chorus at the hour the verse established?
- Is the addressee stable, or did we change targets to save a rhyme?
- Would they use this noun, or did we?
- Is the weird line grain or costume?

Mouth fails after the person is clear → [rhyme-prosody.md](rhyme-prosody.md).
The person is unclear → you do not have a voice problem. You have a LISTEN problem.
