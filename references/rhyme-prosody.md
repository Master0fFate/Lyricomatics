# Rhyme and prosody

Open when the mouth fails. SING is the stage. If it only works on the page, it does not work.

Do not load this file to admire schemes. Load it because a line tripped, a title cannot be belted, or the rhyme bent the syntax into a pretzel.

Density, pocket, and never-do live in `genres/<slug>.md`. This file is the mouth. See genres/ before you decide how much rhyme the pocket will pay for.

## Rhyme is a pressure, not a trophy

Listeners forgive slant more than they forgive clumsy stress. Dense multis impress less than one clean turn if the pocket is not rap.

### Perfect

Same vowel, same closing consonant. *shift / lift*. A lock.

Use it where the pocket wants a hit: chorus end-word, drill end-stop, a punchline landing. Do not force it in a verse that wants to talk.

### Slant

Family rhyme, near. *room / soon*, *shift / left*. Color without the click.

If the perfect rhyme requires inverted syntax, take the slant. Amateur is stress fighting the beat, not a vowel that didn’t quite match.

### Internal

Rhyme inside the line, not only at the fence.

Groove music can live on internals and let the end-word breathe. Rap often wants both. Pop verses can hide a little internal and still sound like speech.

Internals are not an excuse to stuff every syllable. Traffic has to swing.

### Assonance

Vowel color. *soul / home*, *stay / ache*.

Useful on held notes and in sung pockets that hate dense consonant rhyme. A chorus can be “in tune with itself” on vowels without locking every end.

### Consonance

Consonant echo. *back / black / brick*. Drill and punk like the hit. Soft pop often doesn’t.

Do not chain consonance until the line is a drum solo with leftovers of meaning.

No toy charts. If you cannot hear the pressure, speak two options and keep the one that still sounds like the person.

## End-word is the meaning word

The last stressed word in the line should be the thing you meant. Not a rhyme-slave particle. Not *of*, *to*, *it*, *be* wearing a hat.

```
all this pain I’m thinking of
→ I still keep the shirt you hate
```

Write the meaning word first. Rhyme backward from it. Speech order stays speech order. Rhyme-first Yoda is a BLEED kill. [anti-slop.md](anti-slop.md).

## Identical rhyme

*gone / gone*, *love / love*.

Lazy in a story verse. Architecture in a mantra: house, gospel vamp, drill chant, a post that stamps. If the repeat *is* the form, identical is a choice. If you landed there because you ran out of partners, you shrugged.

## Stress to the beat

Default: stressed speech syllables want strong musical beats. Accidental mis-stress of a meaning word sounds amateur.

Intentional displacement is legal — behind the beat, ahead, jazz lag, trap lean, neo-soul drag. The crime is *accidental* Yoda-stress, not pocket.

Speak the line as the character. Where a fist would fall is a candidate for the hit. If the meaning-word is off the hit and the pocket is not syncopated, move the words, not the person’s accent.

Chorus syllable count should be stabler than verse. Verses can limp and recover. Hooks need to be recognizable on pass one. Mantra pockets may loop.

Cut 20% of the syllables on a hook that cannot be caught. Clever is not catchable.

## Speak-test (and page-test)

If you cannot say it in character in one breath chunk, do not rhyme it prettier. Rewrite.

SING prefers out loud, under your breath, tired. If you cannot speak it, do this on the page:

1. Mark the stressed syllables of the meaning words.
2. Split breath groups with `/`.
3. Check that each group is one breath in the pocket.
4. Flag clustered consonants on a title that would be held or shouted.

Without a supplied melody, singability is provisional. Do not reject a line solely because you cannot hear a band.

Mark failures:

- Tongue-twister on a shout pocket (punk, hardcore, drill)
- Clustered consonants on a belted title (*strengths*, *scripts*, stacked /t/ /k/ /s/)
- A hook you have to *read* to understand in a radio pocket
- Subordinate clause that dies before the downbeat in an oral pocket

Fragments are legal. Essay grammar in oral pockets is not. [voice.md](voice.md).

## Vowels that hold

Sustained sung titles want vowels a singer can stay on: *ah*, *oh*, *ay*, and often *ee* even though it is a close vowel. The issue is sustain and release, not a phonetics quiz.

Clustered consonants and clipped vowels belong on the fast bits — chatter, pickup, inside of the bar.

```
Title on a hold: “stay,” “go,” “mine,” “call”
Title that dies on a hold: “strict,” “glimpse,” “object”
```

Rap and drill may not hold. Then the rule flips: clipped ends, hard consonants, few vowels hanging past the kick. Don’t write melisma into a shout.

## Density — don’t guess, open the pocket

See `genres/<slug>.md`. Rough orientation only:

- **Hip-hop / rap** — high pressure. Multis and internals matter. Trap can thin the fence if pocket and punch carry. Drill wants locked end-hits.
- **Pop** — medium. Clean ends on the hook; verses may half-rhyme. Forced perfect in the verse sounds toy.
- **R&B / soul** — low to medium. Vowel color and space for runs. Over-rhyme feels try-hard unless the lane is rap-sung.
- **Country / folk** — chorus can lock; verses may narrative-slant. Dialect-breaking clever rhyme is fatal.
- **Rock / punk** — slogan and hit. Punk would rather shout a slant than pretzel a perfect.
- **Metal** — end-hits and myth-name locks, or no rhyme at all if the line is a blade. Don’t write pop couplets onto a harsh vocal.
- **Blues** — AAB sense-rhyme; the third line pays. Cute multi is the wrong flex.
- **Jazz / theatre** — sense and singability first; scheme is optional and must not smell like a crossword.
- **Gospel** — testimony and vamp phrase; identical as mantra is legal; Latinate sermon-stack is not automatically holy.
- **Dance / electronic** — mantra may barely rhyme. Do not novelize it to “earn” the repeat.
- **Latin, Caribbean, Afro, East Asia** — follow the genre file. Spanish assonance, language mix, and honorifics are not English toy-rhyme. Don’t fake them.

Unknown pocket: do not invent a density. LISTEN first.

## Tiny tests

- Speak it. Does the fist land on the meaning?
- Is the end-word the point, or a slave?
- Would a tired person catch the hook?
- Did you invert syntax to buy a perfect?
- Did you write rap density into a sung bedroom, or essay rhyme into a shout?
