# Examples

Open when you need a before/after, not a lecture. Invented lines only. Not catalog quotes. Not a second song.

Use these as a knife angle. Then write *their* room. Do not paste these into a draft as decoration.

Pattern names match [anti-slop.md](anti-slop.md). Spine: [craft.md](craft.md).

---

### Abstract-noun stacking

**Before:** This aching authenticity, my truth, my hunger for freedom.
**After:** I rehearse the voicemail. I don’t send it.

The stack had no room. The voicemail does.

### Motivational-poster diction

**Before:** Rise up, believe, let your fire light the night.
**After:** I took the raise. I lost Tuesdays.

Cost, timestamp, embarrassment. The poster cannot survive a timesheet.

### Rhyme-first inverted syntax

**Before:** For the crown I fight tonight.
**After:** I’m fighting for the shift.

Speech order first. Meaning word at the fence. Do not gloss the metaphor in the next clause.

### List-of-feelings chorus (pop)

**Before:** I feel broken, I feel lucky, I feel so alive.
**After:** Don’t text. The porch light’s already off.

One claim, one image, one verb. The feelings are implied. Chorus is employed.

### Empty flex (hip-hop)

**Before:** Money, power, I run the whole city.
**After:** Vault’s a storage unit off 20. I still lose the key.

Logistics of the flex. A city name with no predicate would have been the same crime.

### Signifier bingo (country)

**Before:** Pickup, whiskey, dirt road, sunset in my soul.
**After:** Third-shift at the packing plant. Rain in the wheel well.

Job and weather-as-work. The costume closet is not a county.

### Over-explained metaphor (R&B)

**Before:** You’re a storm, because my heart is weather I can’t trust.
**After:** You left the windows open. The floor’s still wet.

Cut the gloss. The floor is the vehicle. Bedroom voice does not file a simile.

### Moral sticker ending

**Before:** And that’s why you should never give up on love.
**After:** I keep your mug. I don’t use it.

End on the object. Let them keep the moral if they need one.

---

## How to use

One move per pass. Don’t stack all eight into a single couplet and call it BLEED.

If the pocket is mantra, do not “improve” a loop with story-detail from the country example. If the pocket is drill, do not soften the after-line into porch-light pop.

When their line fails, write a new after in *their* image system — not a copy of these.
