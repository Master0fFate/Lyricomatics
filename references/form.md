# Form

Open when a section does not know its job, when you are about to bolt on a bridge from habit, or when title placement is turning into confetti.

Form is architecture, not a skeleton you photocopy onto every brief. Label only the rooms the song actually uses. Sheet conventions: [delivery.md](delivery.md).

If the pocket is known, that genre file wins on typical shape. This file still wins on *why a section exists*.

## Section jobs

### Verse

Camera. Proof. New information.

Puts a body in a place with a clock. Does not unemployed the chorus by claiming the whole case in line two.

V2 is not V1 in a different shirt. Camera, time, or power must move. [craft.md](craft.md).

### Pre

Lift and withhold.

Raises pressure, withholds the title one bar longer if the title is a punch. Must not already say the chorus.

If the pre is a smaller chorus, you have two choruses and no lift. Cut or rewrite.

### Chorus

Claim. The case the verses earned.

Stable enough to recognize. Syllable count tighter than the verses. Title lives here unless the form is refrain-verse or mantra-as-vamp.

May tell. Must not inventory feelings. One job: plead, flex, confess, command, mourn, seduce, indict — pick.

### Post

Brand stamp. Earworm stump.

A short leftover after the chorus — title echo, nonsense catch, one-line consequence. Architecture in pop, dance, some rap. Padding everywhere else.

Do not write a second thesis in the post. It is a stump, not a paragraph.

### Bridge

Inform or invert. New information, time jump, opposite POV, or one secret.

Not bigger adjectives on the chorus. Not a moral. Not mandatory (see below).

If you have nothing the other sections didn’t spend, you do not have a bridge. You have nerves.

### Outro

Exit. Image, fade-repeat, last uninterpreted speck, a door closing.

Not a lesson. Not a new plot. Gospel and dance may refuse an outro and vamp instead — that is a form choice, not a missing ending.

### Vamp

Repetition as the destination.

Gospel, some R&B, house, praise-and-worship: the lead phrase is the form. New ad-lib color, same spine. Do not “develop” a vamp into a novella.

### Hook / refrain / break

Use the noun the pocket uses. Drill and rap often want `[Hook]` not a Broadway `[Chorus]`. Punk may be all refrain. Jazz and theatre may not want any of these labels. Don’t invent a pop skeleton for a two-line mantra.

## Four shapes (pick one, don’t soup them)

### Radio

Verse–pre–chorus, optional post, optional bridge tilt, chorus as brand.

Title early enough to tattoo, not stuffed into every bar. Information budget still applies — radio is not permission to restate.

Want-songs in theatre can *look* like radio and still need an objective in a room. Don’t confuse catchy with empty.

### Story

Verse-heavy. Chorus as verdict, proverb, or place-name. Folk, country, some rap, blues.

The verses carry the film. A final-verse twist often beats a rented bridge. Strophic forms may repeat music under new text — that is still story, not mantra.

Do not starve a story song of proper nouns and then call it “universal.”

### Mantra

Few unique lines. The repeat *is* the record.

Dance-floor, gospel vamp, drill chant, some punk slogans, some indie one-liners. Explain-the-mantra is a crime. Verses, if any, are color and must not turn the loop into a short story.

### Through-composed

New text throughout. Theatre scenes, art-song, some folk and jazz.

“Chorus” may be the wrong noun. You still need a governing image and an information budget. Through-composed is not a license to ramble.

Hybrids: primary shape + secondary pocket. Country-trap is story or radio with rap density, not both novels at once.

## When not to force a bridge

Habit writes bridges. Records often don’t need them.

Skip the bridge when:

- The pocket is mantra — house, peak-time dance, drill chant. Give them a break, a drop, a post, a vamp. Not an essay.
- Punk / hardcore — slogan and indictment. A bridge that suddenly “goes thoughtful” is a costume.
- Story song with a last-verse twist already in hand
- The radio draft already has a post that does the leftover job
- You cannot name the new information in one clause

Write a bridge when something actually turns: the other person’s mouth, a time jump, the secret, the cost arriving.

A sentiment-only bridge is a BLEED kill. [anti-slop.md](anti-slop.md).

## Repetition is architecture

Dance, gospel vamp, drill, house, some pop posts: repeating is the job, not a failure of imagination.

Do not “fix” a mantra by adding unique verses until it resembles a singer-songwriter midtempo. You did not make it smarter. You broke the floor.

Rules for repeats that don’t rot:

- One image that can take the loop (CAST named it)
- Tiny state-change if anything changes — the jacket moves, the lights stay, the name gets said harder
- Stable chorus syllable count so the body can land
- No gloss. The tenth pass should not explain the first

If a repeat *does* rot, the line was never strong. Rewrite the spine. Don’t decorate the rot.

## Title inside the form

- **Verse:** almost never, unless refrain-verse or the title is a spoken object in the scene
- **Pre:** withhold, or tease a fragment
- **Chorus:** land it
- **Post:** stamp it, don’t thesis it
- **Bridge:** twist it or refuse it
- **Vamp:** *be* it

Early enough to brand. Not so constant it numbs. Punch titles can delay. Mantra titles can occupy the whole hook — still surround them with a consequence line at least once.

## Tiny tests

- Can you say each section’s job in one clause?
- Does any room duplicate another’s spend?
- Did you add a bridge because the shape looked naked on the page?
- If this is mantra, did you violate it with plot?
