# Anti-slop

The BLEED knife. Open on every genre-known draft, and on any lyric that smells like a caption.

Not a scorecard. Not a gate you announce. A forensic pass: name the residue, cut it, move on.

Each pattern below is **signal → move**. Tiny original lines only. If you need a full before/after, [examples.md](examples.md). If they asked for notes instead of ink, [critique.md](critique.md).

Run [economy.md](economy.md) before this knife. A short generic song is still generic; a long excellent story is not automatically bloated.

Insult the lazy line. Keep the user’s diction. Polish that “upgrades” them into essay English is also slop.

## Nouns that never sat in a room

### Abstract-noun stacking

**Signal:** A pile of -tion / -ness / -ity / “pain, truth, desire, freedom.” No object you could hold. No time of day.

**Move:** Swap the stack for one charged concrete from this narrator’s week. Delete two adjectives to make room.

```
This aching authenticity of my resilience
→ I rehearse the voicemail. I don’t send it.
```

### Motivational-poster diction

**Signal:** Could be printed over a sunset. Rise, soar, believe, light, journey, “never look down.”

**Move:** Add cost, embarrassment, or a timestamp. Kill the poster verbs.

```
Chase your light and never look down
→ I took the raise. I lost Tuesdays.
```

### Latinate diction in oral forms

**Signal:** Utilize, reside, destiny, eternity, transcend, reciprocity — in rap, country, dancehall, punk, bar-room rock.

**Move:** Small verbs. Live, die, pay, leave, want. If the character would not say it at a bus stop, it does not go on the record.

```
I reside in destiny’s embrace
→ I still pay the late fee in cash.
```

### Tonight / fire / heart / dreams / shadows graveyard

**Signal:** Two or more of that set doing the image work. The song is a costume shop.

**Move:** Ban the set for one draft. Force workplace, body logistics, weather-as-work, money, a stain, a time crumb.

```
Your fire in my heart tonight
→ Your shift ends at two. I still heat the plate.
```

### Aesthetic adjective fog

**Signal:** Neon, velvet, infinite, broken light, chrome dusk — especially indie, metal, trip-hop, “cinematic” pop. A chain of pretty modifiers, no object changing state.

**Move:** One object motif, recurring, dirtied or moved. The fog was hiding that you had no noun.

```
Infinite velvet dusk in broken neon
→ The motel ice machine still eats quarters.
```

### List-of-feelings choruses

**Signal:** “I feel X, I feel Y, I feel Z.” A bullet list with a melody.

**Move:** One claim + one image + one action verb. Feelings are implied by the verb, not inventoried.

```
I feel broken, I feel lucky, I feel so alive
→ Don’t text. The porch light’s already off.
```

## Arcs that graduated from workshop

### Symmetrical therapy-arcs

**Signal:** V1 wound / V2 healing / bridge wisdom / chorus triumph. The narrator received a certificate.

**Move:** Break the symmetry. V2 complicates. Leave a scab. End without a graduation speech. See [craft.md](craft.md) on V2 and cost.

```
I was lost, then I healed, now I’m whole
→ I sleep. I still check the chain on the door.
```

### Acreage without motion

**Signal:** A verse exceeds the lane’s normal patience, but every extra line supplies another example of the same fact. The writer mistakes more proof for more feeling.

**Move:** Name the beat in each line. Keep the strongest proof, then move time, place, evidence, knowledge, power, or consequence. Cut the line after the payoff. See [economy.md](economy.md).

### Verse isomorphism

**Signal:** V2 is V1 with the nouns swapped. Same time, evidence, knowledge, power, and rhyme map.

**Move:** Change at least one axis and let the same hook acquire a new meaning.

If you can describe V2 as “the other color dress,” you did not write V2.

### Title-stuffing

**Signal:** Title in every line of chorus, plus pre, plus post. The ear goes numb.

**Move:** Land the title on the payoff bar. Surround it with consequence. Vary once with a twist line. Mantra pockets may repeat — still do not clone the title into the verse as filler.

### Sentiment-only bridge

**Signal:** The bridge restates the chorus with bigger adjectives. New header, same fortune cookie.

**Move:** New information, time jump, opposite POV, or one secret fact. If you have nothing new, you do not have a bridge. See [form.md](form.md) — many records want a post or a break, not an essay.

### Cost-free win

**Signal:** The brief is selling invulnerability — triumph as an ad, no friction, no body. Not: they asked for praise, a party, a joke, or uncomplicated joy.

**Move:** If the song is pretending nothing has a bill, put a small ugly price *they already implied*. Do not invent an absentee, a funeral, or an empty chair to “humanize” a celebration they wanted clean.

```
We made it, now we shine
→ I got the house. I still eat over the sink.
```

If they asked for a sold-out night with no hangover, write the sold-out night. Cost is not a moral tax.

### Moral sticker ending

**Signal:** Last line is the lesson. “That’s why you never…” Family is everything. Believe in you.

**Move:** End on image, action, or an unanswered question. Let the listener keep the moral if they need one.

```
And that’s why you should never give up on love
→ I keep your mug. I don’t use it.
```

## Mouth that isn’t speech

### Perfect grammar in oral forms

**Signal:** Essay clauses in rap, country, dancehall, punk, gospel testimony. Subordinate piles. No fragment where a person would fragment.

**Move:** Speak it. Cut the subordinate clause. Restore the contractions and chips of the *actual* register — faithfully. Costume dialect is a different crime (below).

### Rhyme-first inverted syntax

**Signal:** Yoda for a perfect end-word. “For the crown I fight tonight.” Speech would never order it that way.

**Move:** Write sense in speech order first. Rhyme from the meaning word backward. If the perfect rhyme requires a pretzel, take the slant. Stress crimes are worse than slant. See [rhyme-prosody.md](rhyme-prosody.md).

```
For the crown I fight tonight
→ I’m fighting for the shift.
```

### Fake dialect

**Signal:** Random AAVE, Patois, “country” phonetic spellings, slang salad from three neighborhoods and a TV show. The writer does not live in that mouth.

**Move:** Match the register on the brief. If you do not have it, write plain correct speech in the user’s language. Never cosplay. When uncertain, ask or stay put — [voice.md](voice.md), [ethics.md](ethics.md).

## Heat with no object

### Over-explained metaphors

**Signal:** Image, then the gloss. “Because my heart is like…” You wrote the picture and then captioned it.

**Move:** Cut the because. Trust one vehicle. If it is unclear, sharpen the object, don’t annotate.

```
You’re a storm, because my heart is weather I can’t trust
→ You left the windows open. The floor’s still wet.
```

### Missing idiosyncrasy

**Signal:** No behavior, verbal habit, charged object, relationship dynamic, unusual comparison, local pressure, or unfair detail only this narrator would notice. Anyone could have written it.

**Move:** Add one or two specifics from the brief’s world and cut generic adjectives to pay for them. A proper noun is one option, not a tax. Idiosyncrasy can live in syntax, humor, omission, or what the narrator refuses to do.

### Unexplained proper-noun dumps

**Signal:** Cities, cars, designers, famous corners with no predicate. Fake-flex geography. “From Tokyo to Paris to the block.”

**Move:** Earn each name with a relation, or cut to one loaded name. A proper noun with no job is still abstract.

```
Whole map on my wrist, Tokyo to the block
→ I still owe the guy who fronts me the chain.
```

### Empty flex

**Signal:** Money, power, status with no texture. “I’m that guy. Whole city knows.”

**Move:** Show the awkward logistics of wealth, or the bill of poverty. The vault that is a storage unit. The raise that costs Tuesdays.

### Generic rebellion

**Signal:** “Against the system” with no system. Fist in the air, no landlord, no algorithm, no church board, no sergeant.

**Move:** Name the rule, the boss, the app, the board. Punk still needs an indictment with a target. Drill still needs a room.

### Fluent but ownerless hook

**Signal:** The chorus is concise, repetitive, and perfectly in-genre, but could be transferred to ten neighboring songs without changing a word.

**Move:** Keep the fluent frame. Replace one central noun, verb, premise, or turn with the song’s unfair advantage. Familiar form, distinctive content. Do not make the whole hook strange to prove you are original.

### Prop-inventory realism

**Signal:** Receipts, keys, apps, street names, stains, and timestamps appear because the writer learned “be concrete,” not because any item changes the pressure.

**Move:** Keep the object that acts, changes state, or reveals character. Cut the auditioning furniture. Concrete clutter is abstract writing with better lighting.

## Machine husk (notes and lyrics)

BLEED is the scan. Do not load a second humanizer skill. These tells hit **offered notes and CAST** as hard as the lyric sheet. If the wrap around the song sounds like a model, the room already failed.

### Em dash as gravitas

**Signal:** Stacked em dashes (—) as fake sophistication in lyrics, CAST, or OFFER. One pause in a theatre line can be a breath. Three in a verse is a blog.

**Move:** Period, comma, line break, or nothing. In notes to the user, write like a person in a room. Do not ban a single dash the writer planted on purpose.

### Essay husks in the mouth

**Signal:** *delve, tapestry, moreover, furthermore, amidst, utilize, it is important to note.* "It is not X. It is Y." on every other line. Semicolon essays. Bold or italic inside a lyric line as if markdown were a singer.

**Move:** Cut the husk. Speech order. If the pocket is oral, fragments beat thesis couplets.

### Caption parallelism

**Signal:** Every line the same syntactic stencil. Anaphora that never breaks. A chorus that could be a product landing page.

**Move:** Break one line on purpose. Keep the hook catchable, not identical.

## Moves that pay rent

Keep these on the bench. They are how most of the cuts above actually happen.

- Concrete substitution — abstract becomes a handleable object
- Cost in the win — only if the toast was selling invulnerability; never a tax on joy they asked for
- Leftover speck — one image you refuse to interpret
- Register match — vocabulary = class, region, era, pocket
- Dominant image world — related objects or one motif changing state; a counter-image only for a deliberate turn
- Verse-as-camera / chorus-as-claim
- Time crumb — Tuesday, after the funeral, payday, 3 a.m.
- Body logistics — throat, shoes, sweat, not “soul”
- Power flip between verses
- Cut 15–25% of an overfull first draft; protect the narrative turn and human grain
- Say it wrong once — an intentional fragment for grain
- Latinate → small verb in oral pockets
- Title delay — withhold until the ear needs the brand
- Anti-isomorphism — force V2’s skeleton off V1’s

## Record bar

Silent internal pass. Do not print the score unless the user asks for an evaluation.

Score each 0–2:

1. **Brief fit** — correct job, pocket, era, voice, bans, and emotional purpose.
2. **Memory** — the title/hook/refrain is retrievable after one pass and has an owner.
3. **Economy** — every section earns its length; no duplicate proof or fake bridge.
4. **Motion** — sections change pressure, knowledge, power, participation, or meaning.
5. **Voice** — diction, behavior, humor, and omission belong to this narrator.
6. **Prosody** — natural speech stress, useful end-words, breathable phrases; exact musical fit only when music exists.
7. **Originality** — no lifted skeleton, default premise, mixed-image accident, or prop cosplay.
8. **Aftereffect** — at least one line, turn, or recurrence leaves emotional residue appropriate to the brief. Clean joy may leave lift; comedy may leave the laugh; not every song needs a wound.

A 0 in brief fit, memory spine, originality/ethics, or supplied-melody prosody is fatal. Revise it; do not average it away. Otherwise target 13/16 or better. A number cannot bless a dead hook, and a dense rap verse is not penalized merely for being dense when its turns pay rent.

## Hallway skeptic

Read it cold, out loud, as if you did not write it.

If a line could caption a stock photo, it dies — unless the pocket is a chant and the caption *is* the hook.
If abstracts do all the work in a scene-based story, replace some with behavior or evidence.
If a verse that needs proof has no charged behavior, object, place, or relation — you wrote a mood. Mantra, praise, and house loops are allowed to be spare.
If a verse exceeds budget and you cannot name the new beat in each extra line — cut.
If V2 does not change time, place, evidence, knowledge, power, or consequence — you did not write V2.
If a tired person cannot catch the hook, it is not a hook.
If the hook could be moved intact to ten songs in the lane, it has fluency but no owner.
If the last line is a lesson they did not ask for, throw the lesson, keep the image.
If you would be embarrassed to defend this against the pocket’s never-do list, you already know.
Do not inject grief to pass this hallway.
If CAST or OFFER is full of em dashes and "it is not X, it is Y," rewrite the wrap before you defend the song.

Do not announce this pass. Do it. Then OFFER.
