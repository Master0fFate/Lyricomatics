# Post-Punk and Goth

A flat voice saying something alarming. The lyric is pressure, not decoration: radical content in a plain mouth, one image system, no wink.

## Sub-lanes

- **Post-punk** — spoken-sung, near-monotone declamation. Radical content in radical form, alarming lines delivered flat. Line ends take a stressed syllable or a dropped *ah*. Words fitted to music already written; repetition is pressure.
- **Goth rock** — deep droning baritone. Romantic and liturgical imagery used with discipline. Dread as climate, slogans a crowd intones.
- **Darkwave and coldwave** — synth cold, few words, one sentence of estrangement repeated. European detachment as a stance, not an accent.
- **Industrial and EBM** — shouted or processed commands, mechanical repetition, political or bodily dread. Imperative mood.
- **Synthwave with vocals** — an 80s-shaped hook drenched in reverb. Neon only if a car and a night exist in the song.

A fist-and-enemy shout is `punk.md`. Confession with a learnable chorus is `emo.md`; a dance-floor mantra of yearning is `electronic.md`; harsh or clean metal is `metal.md`.

## Form

**Cold default:** 2–6 declaimed phrases per verse, 0–2 in a pre (rare), 1–4 in a refrain or slogan; industrial runs 2–4 command lines. See [../economy.md](../economy.md).

Verse and refrain, or a verse that builds by repetition. The refrain is often a slogan or a single stated sentence, intoned. If the music exists, it is law: fit the riff, do not make the riff fit the words.

Repetition is pressure. By the third pass the repeated line should mean more because the verses made it worse, not because someone explained it.

V2 worsens the climate. Same room, colder, more people in it, a door that was open now shut. It does not explain V1.

Bridge is usually absent. A break, or a drop to one voice, is enough. A tidy resolving bridge is a kill. Industrial escalates by repeating the command; darkwave can be one sentence and a change of one word. Declamation tires fast, so keep verses short.

## Rhyme pressure

Low to medium. Post-punk is often unrhymed; the pattern lives in the stressed or dropped end of each line. Goth lets a rhyme sound like a hymn, so lock it on the refrain only. Industrial rhymes by consonant hit, not by vowel. Synthwave can pay a tidy hook rhyme, 80s-style.

Forced rhyme sounds like gothic pastiche: the dark/stark reach, the night/light reflex. Inversion is the tell. A Romantic "forsaken am I" in a flat mouth is costume, not menace.

Write the claim in speech order. Then put the heavy word at the fence.

## Pocket / mouth

The declaimed line must be sayable flat, with no emphasis, and still land. Word order and consonant weight carry the meaning, so the verb sits where a lift would have gone and the noun is heavy.

Baritone holds want open vowels at the fence (ah, oh, aw). Closed vowels sag in a droning register. Goth lines can be long and low; post-punk lines are clipped and spoken, ending on a stressed syllable or a dropped *ah*. Industrial wants hard consonants and short words that survive processing. Darkwave wants few words, vowel-led.

Synthwave hooks need open vowels on the held note and a plain 80s-shaped phrase.

Speak test: say it flat at conversation speed in a cold room, with no lift. If it needs a sneer, a rise, or a sigh to mean anything, the word order is wrong.

## Flow parity

Verses **tight**: declamation sits on a grid even when it sounds like talk, so the stress count and the line ending must match. Slogans and refrains **strict**. The held baritone vowel matches at the fence on every return.

Industrial commands: **strict** blow count, same consonant hit on the same beat.

Nothing here runs **off**. Flat delivery sounds free and still sits on the music's grid; that is why it works.

Trap: a declaimed line that adds a clause in verse two because flat delivery seemed to allow it.

## POV & voice

The speaker reports and does not emote: a witness, an official, an inmate, a body, a crowd. Second person is a command or an accusation. "We" is a crowd intoning, never a youth campaign. First person is estranged and short on adjectives.

Register is clerical, liturgical, or clinical. Plain nouns, small verbs. One image system per song (the building, the body, the state), never a gothic junk drawer.

Liturgical language is borrowed for its structure, call and answer and litany, not as church furniture. Irony is in the delivery, not a wink on the page. Detachment is a stance. Do not fake a foreign accent or sprinkle foreign phrases; if the brief supplies another language, ask for a competent speaker on it.

## Hook

An intoned slogan. Short, one skeleton, sayable by a crowd with no melody to lean on. Darkwave's hook is one sentence of estrangement repeated; industrial's is a command; synthwave's is a clean 80s-shaped phrase with a real car or night behind it.

It must survive a flat delivery and a droning crowd. It must not resolve the dread, name a mood, or list gothic nouns.

## Kill list

- Ravens, graveyards, black roses
- "Eternal night"
- Cathedral as the default room
- "The void" without an object
- Lace and candles costume
- Neon-and-rain postcard synthwave
- Latin phrases as gravitas
- A tidy resolving bridge
- Adjective fog with no noun changing state
- Irony winked on the page

## AI failure modes

The gothic junk drawer: raven, mirror, rose, and bell in one verse. A speaker who emotes where it should report. Ornate lines that cannot be said flat. Two image systems fighting. A V2 that explains V1. Dread named and never enacted. Industrial that lists slogans with no body behind them. Synthwave with neon and no car. Rhyme reaching for darkness. Romantic inversion as a voice. A bridge that closes the climate.

If a line needs a sneer to land, or the image system changes between verses, it is not done.

## Briefing questions

1. Which lane: declaimed post-punk, droning goth, cold darkwave, industrial command, or synthwave hook?
2. What is the one image system (a building, a body, or a state), and what does V2 do to it?
3. Which line is the slogan, and who intones it?

## Hybrid note

Primary when flat delivery of a controlled image system, a crowd-intoned slogan, or one cold repeated sentence leads. `punk.md` donates its kill list (rebellion with no target, essay punk) when a living enemy exists; `rock.md` donates aesthetic adjective fog; `electronic.md` donates no-novel-on-a-loop and no-tidy-sermon-ending for darkwave, EBM, and synthwave; `metal.md` donates its gothic mad-lib ban. A one-breath fist-slogan with a named enemy is `punk.md` primary. Goth-metal with a harsh or clean metal throat and a held doctrine is `metal.md` primary. Do not average a declaimed verse with a chorus the room sings back; pick which one the room remembers.
