# Blues

Trouble with a punchline. The repeated line is a machine, not laziness. Wit lives in the wound or the wound is tourism.

## Sub-lanes

- **Classic AAB** — line, repeat or lean-variation, response that turns or deepens. Repetition is architecture.
- **Narrative couplets** — 12-bar story talk; still a complaint with a body, a bill, a name, a place.
- **Modern blues-rock hybrid** — only if the brief demands it. Do not secretly convert AAB into a pop chorus because you got bored.

Juke shout vs solo confession is a delivery choice. Ask. It changes how much air and joke the page needs.

## Form

AAB is the default spine. A states the trouble. A-repeat insists. B pays off, twists, jokes, or deepens. If B only paraphrases A with a synonym for sorrow, you did not write a B.

Repetition is not a first-draft leftover. If you are embarrassed to repeat, you are in the wrong pocket or your A line is weak.

Do not drop AAB for verse–chorus pop unless CAST names a modern hybrid and you can say why the refrain needs a separate chorus body.

A later chorus/verse should worsen or complicate the trouble, not heal it on a schedule.

## Rhyme pressure

End-rhyme strong on the payoff line. Slant welcome. The chime is part of the shrug.

Do not over-scheme the A line into a rap bar. It has to be bent, dragged, re-said.

Rhyme-first inversion kills the talk. These lines want to be spoken to a lover, a landlord, the devil, or the self — in that order of likelihood, depending on the brief.

## Pocket / mouth

Swing and drag. Write lines that can be bent. Leave call-and-response gaps — the answer may be a guitar or a room, but the lyric should not fill every hole like a nervous pop verse.

Syllables that can stretch on a vowel survive. Clusters that cannot be complained on a late beat do not.

Humor is traditional, not mandatory. If the brief is a funeral, do not inject a wink. If the brief is a complaint with a grin, a page of misery with no glint is a museum placard.

No minstrel phonetics. No “authentic dialect” invented from vibes. If the brief does not give a register, write plain trouble in clean speech the singer can lean.

## POV & voice

First-person trouble. Address: lover, landlord, devil, self. Second person can tease or accuse. Third person is rarer and should still feel like talk, not reportage from a folklore class.

Concrete trouble: money, jail, cheating, body, train, job, rent, a name that will not leave. Abstract sorrow nouns are pop slop in a blues hat.

The narrator can be wrong. The song should not rush to correct them.

## Hook

The repeated A line *is* the hook machine. Title often the complaint or the place.

If you invent a separate pop chorus, it must still be a complaint you can shout, not an empowerment lift.

Do not be ashamed of recurrence. Recurrence is the form’s intelligence.

## Kill list

- “Woke up this morning” with nothing after
- Misery tourism
- Fake dialect caricature
- Abstract sorrow stacks (pain, loneliness, my soul)
- Dropping AAB for a pop chorus without a hybrid brief
- Pretty grammar that would not survive a room
- Healing final chorus
- Museum blues: trains and crossroads as stickers, no bill
- Joke-free wound presented as purity
- Explaining the trouble twice instead of turning it

## AI failure modes

Generic suffering. No wit. Overwritten into pop sections without cause. Caption-sad. Dialect cosplay. Every B line a moral. No job, no rent, no body. “Authenticity” via keyword checklist. Narrator who has feelings instead of trouble.

If the page would not get a laugh of recognition from someone in actual debt or actual trouble, it is costume.

## Briefing questions

1. What is the concrete trouble — money, jail, cheating, body, job, other?
2. Is humor allowed in the wound?
3. Solo confession or juke-joint shout?
