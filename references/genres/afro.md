# Afro

Cool temperature. Dance-floor breathability. Romance flex, softlife, invitation — not a lecture over a log drum. Languages you were not given do not appear.

## Sub-lanes

- **Afrobeats** — hooks early, verses lean, call-outs and dance cues. English (when English) follows the artist’s actual rhythm of speech, not American radio-ballad arcs.
- **Amapiano** — even more space. Lyrics must not carpet-bomb the pocket. Short hooks, repeated titles, air for chops and ad-libs.

If they want dense 16s, that is a rap feature — `hip-hop.md` for that verse, not an excuse to smother this groove.

## Form

Groove-first. Hook shows up early. Verses stay lean. Dance cues and call-outs are legal when they sound like a person commanding a floor, not a fitness instructor.

Do not write a four-section pop novel. Information budget is small. Repetition is the arrangement’s friend; the lyric should survive it.

V2 is a new flirt, a new flex texture, a later hour — not a second motivational paragraph.

Post-chorus / chant tags are welcome. Bridges that philosophize are usually a mistake.

## Rhyme pressure

Medium-low. Vibe, repetition, and phonetic pleasure beat dense multis unless the brief is a rap feature.

End-rhyme can be light. Identical title hits can be mantra, not laziness.

Do not force English multi-rhyme culture onto a brief that wants assonance and repeat. Do not invert speech to look “lyrical.”

If you are writing English only, make it singable and current to the artist — not essay, not slang-salad from another continent.

## Pocket / mouth

Prioritize breathability. Short hooks. Flexible verse syllables that leave holes.

Amapiano: treat space as a sacred object. If the page looks full, it is wrong.

Avoid dense consonant clusters on hook holds. Prefer vowels that can be chopped, echoed, repeated.

Speak it at a sway, not a cipher speed, unless CAST named a rap verse.

## POV & voice

Romance flex, softlife, party invitation, subtle status. First and second person. Cool, not desperate — unless the brief wants ache, and even then the ache should still dance.

Tourist gaze is banned: “African queen” as a postcard, safari nouns, continent-as-flavor. Write a person in a night, a city, a room, a body.

Brands and flex only if true to the artist’s world. Fake luxury lists are hip-hop slop leaking in.

Code-switch **only** if the user provides the languages and the lines, or the artist brief names languages they actually speak **and** you have competence. Otherwise: English (or the supplied language) without decorative fragments for spice. Do not invent Yoruba, Twi, Zulu, Pidgin, or any other line from vibes.

When English: prefer the artist’s cadence. Do not American-radio the emotion into a therapy arc.

## Hook

Title as lifestyle claim, address, place, or a feeling-word that survives repetition. “My baby,” a city, a swagger noun — if it is theirs.

The hook must leave space for vocal chops and ad-libs. If it is a paragraph, it is pop in the wrong climate.

Dance instructions implied, not barked like aerobics, unless the sub-lane and brief want a commander.

## Kill list

- Generic “African queen” tourist gaze
- Buzzword stuffing without a scene
- English motivational overlay on a groove that wants softness
- Lecture over a log drum
- Dense 16-bar rap unless the brief is a feature
- Fake local-language fragments for spice
- American radio-ballad emotional graduation
- Carpet-bombed pocket
- Status flex with no texture and no truth to artist
- Continent-as-aesthetic, no city, no night

## AI failure modes

Overwritten English pop verses. Cultural keyword confetti. No space. Therapy arc. Invented language garnish. Fitness-instructor dance commands. Identical verses. A hook that dies if repeated eight times. Narrator who sounds like an export-market brochure.

If you needed a translation you guessed, you already violated the pocket.

## Briefing questions

1. Language mix — which languages does the artist actually speak, and what may appear on the page?
2. Softlife romance or dance-floor commander?
3. How much space must the hook leave for chops and ad-libs?

## Hybrid note

Primary when groove-space and cool-temperature hook lead. Afro-pop: stay here if repetition and air win; `pop.md` if the text-message radio thesis wins — then run this kill list against tourist gaze and fake fragments. Afro-rap feature: this file owns the hook; `hip-hop.md` owns the guest verse; never let the verse teach the hook to overeat. Amapiano/electronic: this space rule plus `electronic.md` kill list (no novel on a loop, no rave-spirituality poster). Language policy does not relax in hybrids.
