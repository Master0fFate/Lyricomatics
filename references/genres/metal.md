# Metal

Doctrine, curse, or a place-name of doom. One metaphor domain held for the whole song. Volume of adjectives is not escalation.

## Sub-lanes

- **Melodic / clean-chorus** — sing-along tragedy. Open vowels on the title. Verses may snarl; the hook must still be bellowable.
- **Extreme / harsh** — ritual phrase, percussive consonants, short clauses. Horror-witness or prophet. Breath groups are weapons.
- **Concept / myth** — longer arc allowed if the creature, war, or scripture stays coherent. Lore is a system, not a noun pile.

If the brief is pit-shout hardcore with a living political enemy, check `punk.md` before you costume it as metal.

## Form

Verse / optional pre / chorus. Breakdown lines chunked for rhythmic hits — write them as blows, not sentences that happen to be loud. Extended narrative sections are legal in concept work; they still need a refrain the crowd can occupy.

Escalate image, not synonym density. V2 should worsen the situation, flip victim/vector, or move the war to a new room of the same metaphor house.

A bridge that only says “I am powerful and dark” in new makeup is refuse.

Do not drop a soft-pop middle eight into harsh verses unless the brief wants that contrast on purpose and CAST says so.

## Rhyme pressure

Medium. Alliteration and consonant aggression often outrank cute end rhyme. Clean choruses still want memorable ends.

Harsh vocals: rhyme can be slammed and slanted; the ear is tracking hits and vowels of rage, not crossword elegance.

Do not invert into high-fantasy Yoda to park *abyss* at the barline. Speech order, then the chime.

Lore names are not rhymes. If you invent a proper noun, it must do work in a predicate or it is clutter.

## Pocket / mouth

Match the vocal world named in the brief.

Harsh: percussive consonants, shorter phrases, no tongue-twisters, no hanging perfume vowels past the kick. If it cannot be barked in one breath chunk, split it.

Clean: open vowels, held title words, chorus you can bellow without chewing clusters.

Mixed: do not write the harsh verse in essay English and the clean chorus in poster English. One diction world, two deliveries.

## POV & voice

Epic first or third. Accusatory second. Horror-witness. Unreliable prophet when earned.

Narrator is victim, vector, or god — pick. Mixing all three without a turn is mush.

Coherent domain: war, disease, industry, scripture, machinery, a single myth ecology. Hold it. Do not tour darkness as a gift shop.

Latinate mush (*eternity, destiny, transcend*) is a tell unless the scripture domain truly speaks that way — and even then, prefer verbs that kill, pay, rust, kneel.

## Hook

Title as curse, doctrine, or doomed place. Melodic metal wants sing-along tragedy. Extreme wants a ritual phrase that can be repeated without becoming a motivational growl.

The hook is the doctrine in one breath. Verses are evidence.

## Kill list

- Darkness / shadows / souls / abyss chained as synonyms
- Fantasy noun salad with no ecology
- “I am the night” with no cost
- Lore dump of unexplained proper nouns
- Soft pop therapy inside death-growl verses without intent
- Interchangeable gothic mad-libs
- Chorus that only restates power and gloom
- Aesthetic fog: infinite night, broken light, velvet void
- Random Latin for gravitas
- Victim + god + preacher in one unturned stanza

## AI failure modes

Every song the same haunted mad-lib. No through-line creature or story. Adjective volume mistaken for intensity. Clean chorus that could be any epic-pop title. Harsh verse that is actually a blog about pain. Unexplained names as fake grandeur. No governing metaphor — just weather in a crypt.

If you can replace the title with “Darkness Inside” and lose nothing, you have nothing.

## Briefing questions

1. Clean, harsh, or mixed vocal world?
2. What is the one governing metaphor domain — war, disease, industry, scripture, other?
3. Is the narrator victim, vector, or god?

## Hybrid note

Primary when the doctrine/curse hook and the vocal-world mouth outrank slogan-hardcore or radio-pop stamp. Metalcore with a living social enemy and one-breath slogans: consider `punk.md` as primary and steal this file’s kill list so abyss-synonyms do not leak. Symphonic/pop-metal: stay here for domain coherence; run `pop.md` kill list so the clean chorus does not become an empowerment list. Rap-metal: `hip-hop.md` if bars are the product; otherwise this doctrine hook plus hip-hop’s slang-salad ban.
