# Gospel

Testimony, praise, lament, or an altar call. The core must survive eight minutes of vamp. Choir answers are architecture. Holy nouns are not a costume for a secular template.

## Sub-lanes

- **Testimony** — specific deliverance story; I that becomes we. Scene before slogan.
- **Praise** — names, thanks, declaration. Plain speech that can be shouted. Not a doctrine lecture.
- **Lament / altar-call** — plea with a body in it; invitation that a room can answer. Bridge as declaration that can modulate in performance without needing new paragraphs on the page.

Christian radio love-song with Jesus swapped for baby is pop ethics failure, not this pocket.

## Form

Call-and-response. Verse–chorus–vamp. Testimony verse plus communal refrain.

Vamp: short, repeatable, intensifying lines. If the vamp cannot be layered, you overwrote it. Do not dump original paragraphs into the vamp because you still had theology to cover.

Bridge as modulation-ready declaration — one claim, not an essay. The singer and choir will occupy repetition. Trust that.

A second verse should add a scene or a worse night, not a second slogan.

Do not force a pop pre-chorus unless the brief is contemporary gospel radio and the pre has a lift job that is not already the chorus.

## Rhyme pressure

Medium. Communal clarity beats dense schemes. The room must catch it on first pass.

Clever internals that steal the vowel from the choir are vanity. Save turns for testimony lines that a soloist can land.

Do not invert scripture-ish English into rhyme-Yoda. These lines get shouted by people who did not come to decode you.

## Pocket / mouth

Space for choir answers and ad-libs. Write the spine; leave the holes.

Vamp lines short enough to stack. Open vowels on the held names and titles. Clusters belong in spoken testimony, not on the praise hold.

Call, then air. If the page is a paragraph, the choir has nowhere to stand.

Read it as if a room will repeat the last line until it breaks. If you would be embarrassed by that repetition, the line is too clever or too empty.

## POV & voice

Testimony I. Communal we. Direct address to God. Second person encouragement to the congregation.

Pick the addressee and hold it long enough to mean something. Whiplash between lover-talk and Lord-talk is how secular templates sneak in.

Biblical paraphrase should still sound sung, not quoted. Thrift-store psalm mashups with no through-line are refuse.

Corporate-church “breakthrough” speak is a kill. Prosperity clichés without a testimony scene are a kill. Specific deliverance — what was the night, the bill, the diagnosis, the door — is the craft.

Handle sacred material as a community standard, not a vibe. The brief’s hard lines win. Do not invent denomination-specific doctrine to sound deep.

## Hook

Scriptural plain speech or a praise name. Title that survives a long vamp.

The hook is what the room can say without a sheet. If it needs a footnote, it is a verse.

Do not explain the testimony in the hook. The hook is the claim the story earned.

## Kill list

- Empty “breakthrough” corporate-church speak
- Prosperity clichés without testimony detail
- Psalm-sticker mashup, no through-line
- Swap “baby” for “Jesus” in a secular template
- Overwritten vamp paragraphs
- Doctrine lecture without praise or story
- Motivational poster plus random Lord
- Pop love-song structure with holy nouns swapped in
- No scene in a testimony song
- Choir given nothing short enough to answer

## AI failure modes

Holy nouns on a pop skeleton. Testimony lane with no scene. Praise lane forced into a trauma story. Vamp that is a new essay. Everyone addressed at once. Therapy-gospel (healing arc, graduation speech). Abstract deliverance. Quote-salad scripture. Identical verses. A chorus that could be a gym anthem with one word changed.

If God is a featured guest in a self-help song, you missed the pocket.
