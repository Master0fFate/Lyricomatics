# South Asia

The song serves a scene, a character, or a floor. The hook is one line in one language that a room can return to. Write the English, mark the slots, never fake the rest.

## Sub-lanes

- **Bollywood / filmi** — mukhda, interlude, then two or three antaras, each ending on a connector line that leads back into the mukhda. The mukhda is the hook and the title. Situational: the song serves a scene and a character, often a duet.
- **Punjabi pop and bhangra** — dance hook, boliyan-style short couplets, dhol-driven. Swagger, village-to-city pride. English and Punjabi mix here because the lane does.
- **Hindi indie and Desi hip-hop** — Hinglish verses, gully rap economics, bars that must survive a cypher.
- **Ghazal-influenced film ballad** — radif, qafia, matla, sher. Every couplet closes its own thought.

If the bars are the whole product with no Hinglish or filmi frame, that is `hip-hop.md`. If they want K-pop or J-pop, that is `east-asia.md`.

## Form

**Mukhda default:** mukhda 2–4 sung phrases, each antara 4–6, two or three antaras, one connector line per antara; interludes carry no text. Punjabi dance hook 1–2 phrases, boliyan couplets 2 each. Hinglish verses 8–16 bars, hook 2–4. See [../economy.md](../economy.md).

Filmi runs mukhda, interlude, antara, mukhda, interlude, antara, mukhda. The mukhda states the whole claim in one breath and is the title. The antara earns the return: it moves the scene, then the connector line hands the room back.

Each antara has its own tune and its own job. Antara 1 sets the want or the wound. Antara 2 is the later hour, or the other half of the duet answering. A third antara is the bridge. Do not bolt a pop bridge onto this shape. In a duet, two voices carry two agendas; if both say the same thing in turn, cut one.

Punjabi dance tracks run hook, couplet, hook. Each boliyan couplet is a finished boast or tease with the turn on its second line. Stack them. Do not narrate across them. Hinglish rap takes `hip-hop.md` terms for the grid: hook first, V2 a new camera.

A ghazal-shaped ballad runs matla, then sher after sher. Couplets may be independent; each one closes its own thought, and the order is mood, not plot. The film ballad may borrow the radif and the closed couplet and still return to a mukhda.

## Rhyme pressure

High. Mukhda and antara line ends are the words the room shouts. Hindi, Urdu, and Punjabi verb endings rhyme almost for free, and the free chime shows. Rhyme on the meaning word, not the inflection.

Ghazal: the qafia is a rule. The rhyme sound before the radif lands in every sher, and the radif returns unchanged while its meaning turns. The matla sets both, and both its lines carry them. The machinery works in English too: a radif phrase that means something new each couplet, a qafia you can hear.

Hinglish: rhyme inside the language of the line. A chime that jumps from an English word to a Hindi one is a speaker's call, not yours. Forced rhyme here sounds like a formal-register word dragged into a casual song to catch the chime.

## Pocket / mouth

Hindi and Urdu long vowels hold; short vowels do not. The held note goes on a long vowel. Never park a sustained note on a short one. A competent speaker confirms which vowels are long.

The singer ornaments on long vowels. Fill the line with short syllables and there is nowhere to lean. Leave the stressed ends open.

Punjabi dance: short syllables that land like drum hits, and a hook that survives a loud room. Desi hip-hop: hard and aspirated consonants are percussion, so spend them on the beat. Switch language at a phrase boundary, where the mouth can change gear, never mid-phrase on the beat.

Speak the English portion at tempo. For each native slot, tap the long and short pattern aloud and hand that pattern to the speaker.

## Flow parity

Mukhda **strict** on every return: same syllables, same stress, same ending. The long "aa" and "ee" holds keep their vowel every time. The radif of a ghazal-shaped ballad is the mukhda's cousin: same words, same slot, **strict**.

Antaras **tight** to each other when they share a tune. Classic filmi gives each antara its own tune; then antara 1 line n pairs with antara 2 line n only if the brief says they share music. Otherwise each antara is **off** against the others but **strict** against its own repeat.

Punjabi dance hook **strict**; boliyan couplets **tight**. Desi hip-hop verses are **off** on syllables but owe bar parity: the rhyme lands on the same beat inside one flow pattern. Hook **strict**.

Count syllables in the sung language, not in the English gloss. A long vowel is a held slot; a short vowel is not. Ghazal meter is a pattern of long and short, not a head count, so the speaker supplies the scansion or the slot stays marked.

Trap: a connector line back into the mukhda that changes length between antaras, so the band cannot cue the return.

## POV & voice

Filmi writes for the character on screen, not the singer's diary. Lover to beloved, friend to friend, mother to child, one voice answering another. Punjabi pop boasts, teases, and counts what the move to the city cost. Hindi indie is first person with a rent day, a shift, a named stretch of road.

The agent does not invent Hindi, Urdu, or Punjabi lines. Drafts in those languages need a competent speaker on the brief. The agent writes the English portion, marks each native slot with its syllable count, its held vowel, and its job, or asks. Register is the speaker's call: Urdu-leaning for ghazal diction, plain Hindi for a film love song.

Hinglish mixing has rules. Switch at phrase boundaries. Keep the hook in one language. A Hindi word dropped into an English sentence as spice is the tourist's move.

Gun glamor and caste-pride swagger are the Punjabi lane's known ethics gate; not unless the brief owns them. Qawwali, bhajan, and kirtan are devotional and community-bound. Do not borrow their imagery for costume.

## Hook

The mukhda is the hook and the title. One breath, sung again after every antara, so it must wear well on the fifth pass. It names the feeling of the scene, not the plot.

Punjabi dance hook: a chant the floor shouts over drums, in one language, short enough to survive noise. Hinglish hook: one language, plain, and it still works for a room that never heard the verses.

It must not be an English title with a Hindi garnish, the first line of a verse, or a list of signifiers meant to prove the song is Indian.

## Kill list

- "Namaste" as greeting, sign-off, or title
- "Desi" as the adjective for everything
- Bollywood as a synonym for India
- A Hindi noun dropped into an English hook for flavor
- Sitar, henna, spice market, and Taj postcard imagery with no scene
- Mustard-field and tractor bingo for Punjabi pride, no named place, no job
- A mukhda that is a verse paragraph or has no title
- An antara that restates the mukhda in new adjectives
- A duet where both singers say the same thing
- Ghazal by vibes: no radif, no qafia, couplets that do not close
- Gully rap with no named street, rent, or shift

## AI failure modes

Hinglish by sprinkle: English sentences seasoned with a Hindi noun. Invented Hindi, Urdu, or Punjabi that a speaker would wince at: phrasebook grammar, wrong gender, wrong register. English syllable counts on a Hindi slot. A held note parked on a short vowel. A mukhda with no title and no return value. Antaras that restate. A connector line that does not lead back. Duets with one voice in two costumes. Bollywood reduced to rain, chiffon, and mountains. Ghazal by vibes. Bars written as English poetry with a Hindi ad-lib every fourth line. Punjabi pride as a postcard of fields and tractors.

If you cannot say which language the mukhda is in and who on screen is singing it, it is not done.

## Briefing questions

1. Which lane, and who is the competent speaker for the Hindi, Urdu, or Punjabi lines?
2. What is the scene or the floor, who sings, and do the antaras share one tune?
3. Which language holds the hook, and where does the second language enter?

## Hybrid note

Primary when the mukhda and antara shape, the dance hook, or the Hinglish frame leads. `pop.md` donates its kill list so a dance hook is not an empowerment slogan; `rnb.md` so a film ballad is not a Pinterest chorus; `hip-hop.md` for Desi hip-hop bars, run on the verse only. Filmi with a rap interlude: keep this form, let `hip-hop.md` own that verse's pressure. Where bars are the product, `hip-hop.md` is primary and this file's language rules stay law. Do not average a mukhda with a pop pre-chorus: the pre withholds, the mukhda states, pick one. Never use a hybrid as permission to fake fluency.
