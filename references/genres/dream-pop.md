# Dream Pop

The sound of the word outranks its dictionary meaning. A voice used as texture, one concrete object so the wash has a floor, and a sheet that may be the only place the lyric is ever read.

## Sub-lanes

- **Dream pop and shoegaze** — the vocal is a texture, murmured or smudged into the wall of guitar. Open vowels chosen for sound, fragments, the singer as painter, not poet.
- **Bedroom pop and lo-fi** — close mic, dry, intimate. Soft consonants reduce clarity on purpose. Conversational, often written instrumental-first over a short loop, with the plainness of a text message.
- **Slowcore** — slow, sparse backing that exposes the voice. Plain, melancholic, descriptive lines with long silences between phrases. No ornament.
- **Ambient pop** — one image held across a long form.

A strange image with a band throat and a shirt-print chorus is `rock.md`. Confession with gang vocals is `emo.md`. A club loop with a sentence of yearning is `electronic.md`.

## Form

**Spare default:** 2–4 sung phrases per verse, 0–1 in a pre, 1–4 in a chorus or refrain; ambient pop may spend the whole song on one image. See [../economy.md](../economy.md).

Phrases are short and often incomplete. A fragment can be a whole line. Write to the loop if there is one: the words fit what repeats, and the sections change when the sound changes, not when the story does.

Repeat a fragment and change one word. That single swap is the plot of a verse in this family. If the changed word changes nothing, the repetition is wallpaper.

V2 moves one object, one hour, or one light. It does not deepen the argument or explain V1. The room is the same room, an hour later.

Bridge is usually absent. A drop to a single phrase, or a long instrumental stretch, does the job. A bridge that explains the mood is the failure. Slowcore leaves silence between phrases and fills none of it. Ambient pop holds the image and changes one word on the last return.

## Rhyme pressure

Low. Vowel echo and repetition do the work that end-rhyme does elsewhere. Assonance beats a perfect chime, and a perfect chime that clicks shut kills the drift.

Lock the rhyme, if there is one, on the fragment that returns and the slot that is held. Slowcore can go unrhymed and plain; if it rhymes, the rhyme comes late and quiet.

A tidy forced rhyme in a murmur sounds like a greeting card read aloud in a church. Do not invert a line to catch a vowel. A fragment need not rhyme at all.

## Pocket / mouth

Choose vowels for sound. Open vowels (ah, aw, oh, ay) on holds; close vowels where the smear should narrow. Soft consonants (m, n, l, v, w, h) blur the line on purpose in bedroom pop. Hard stops and clusters are accents. Spend them once.

Shoegaze lines are read on the sheet and heard as colour. Bedroom pop is dry and near: the line is a thing said close to a microphone at an odd hour. Slowcore exposes every syllable and every breath, so a lazy line has nowhere to hide.

Short lines. Give each phrase room on both sides. Do not fill the quiet with a second line that restates the first.

Speak test: murmur it at half volume with your eyes shut. If you hear the consonants before the vowels, the line is too hard for the wash. If it is clear and bland, it is a caption. Slowcore: say each phrase alone in a quiet room, then wait. Any phrase that cannot stand alone goes.

## Flow parity

Verse pairs **tight** on syllables, any fragment that returns **strict**, and **full vowel parity**: every stressed slot keeps its vowel family across the pair, not only the hold, because the vowel is the instrument. Open stays open, close stays close, short stays short.

Slowcore is **strict**. One extra syllable is audible for a full second.

Fragments that never return are **off**. They owe only speech stress and a clean breath.

Trap: swapping a close vowel onto a slot the first verse held open, so the smear changes colour.

## POV & voice

I, or you, or a pronoun with no name, murmured to someone who may not be listening. Third person works as a painter's eye: a room described, no verdict. No crowd. No "we" unless two people are in the room.

Register is quiet and plain. Essay grammar (which, whereby, subordinate clauses stacked three deep) cannot be sung in a murmur. Bedroom pop speaks like a message typed at 1 a.m., and plainness is not permission for blandness: one odd, true detail per section.

Each section keeps one concrete object. A kettle, a bus pass, a window with a crack. Colour words are paint, not content.

## Hook

A fragment, a vowel shape, or one image. It must survive being half-heard under guitar. The sheet may be the only place the words are read, so the sound has to hold the song alone.

It carries the concrete object so the wash has a floor. It must not be a tidy chorus that explains the mood. Slowcore's hook is the line you hear alone in the silence.

## Kill list

- Velvet, neon, infinite, haze, dissolve as the vocabulary
- "Like a dream"
- Colours as the whole bank
- "Fading" as the only verb
- A wash with no object
- Essay grammar in a murmur
- A tidy chorus that explains the mood
- Lo-fi sadness with no room or hour
- Repetition with a changed word that changes nothing
- Slowcore padded with a filler line to fill the silence

## AI failure modes

Pretty words with no noun under them. Complete grammatical sentences murmured into a wall. Vowels chosen for dictionary sense and never for the hold. V2 sliding to a different vowel colour. A chorus that resolves the mood. Every fragment repeating identical, or none repeating at all. Colour, light, and water as the entire vocabulary. Bedroom plainness slid to blandness: a missed person with no hour and no room. Slowcore lines stretched to fill the quiet. Every section the same length and the same softness.

If you can delete the one concrete object and nothing changes, it is not done.

## Briefing questions

1. Which lane: wall of guitar, close-mic bedroom, slowcore, or ambient? Is the lyric meant to be read, or only felt?
2. What is the one concrete object the wash stands on?
3. Which fragment returns, and which single word changes?

## Hybrid note

Primary when sound outranks sense and the vocal is texture, near-mic intimacy, or exposed slowness. `rock.md` donates its kill list (aesthetic adjective fog, indie that explains its symbolism); `electronic.md` donates no-novel-on-a-loop and no-tidy-sermon-ending. Dream-pop with a strange image and a real band throat: `rock.md` primary, and this kill list stops the adjective fog. Slowcore confession with a chorus the room sings back is `emo.md`; a bare acoustic diary with ugly stresses is `folk.md`. Do not average a smudged vowel-wash with a shoutable chorus. One of them is the memory spine.
