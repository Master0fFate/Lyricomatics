# Hip-Hop

Speech under a grid. Reputation in the mouth. If it cannot be said by this narrator this week, it is not a bar.

## Sub-lanes

- **Boom-bap** — dense internals, busy mid-bar, punch that reverses a setup. End words must land like verdicts.
- **Trap** — air as craft. Mood plus one flex noun. Room for ad-libs is structure, not leftover.
- **Drill** — chant hook, short lethal couplets, clipped consonants, threat as climate not costume.
- **Conscious / abstract** — monologue that earns extra length. Thesis in plain speech. Image chains allowed; seminar diction is not.

Name the sub-lane in CAST. Do not blend all four into a tour of “rap.” UK drill, grime, and garage MC work have their own mouth: `uk-rap.md`.

## Form

**Grid default:** 8, 12, or 16 bars when the brief calls for a counted verse; hook often 2–8 bars. A page line is not automatically a bar. Internal turns should pay rent every 4 bars. See [../economy.md](../economy.md).

Default muscle: verse bodies that feel like sixteen, hook that feels like eight. Counts are felt, not worshipped. Abandon the grid only if the monologue pays rent every four bars.

Boom-bap wants a closed argument with turns. Trap can run a leaner verse if identity lives in pocket and hook. Drill prefers mantra chorus plus couplets over a chapter. Conscious work may stretch; stretching is not rambling.

Second verse is a new camera, clock, or power balance. Find-replace nouns across identical rhyme maps is a corpse.

Hook early enough to brand. Do not restatement-bridge unless the brief is story rap that actually turns.

## Rhyme pressure

High. Multis, internals, and slant chains outrank pretty perfect ends — except drill, which wants the end-hit locked, and boom-bap, which still punishes a lazy last word.

Trap may thin the scheme if pocket and punchline carry. Thin is not empty. Abstract may chain slants across a thought; sound cannot launder a vacant line.

End word is the meaning word. Particles hanging off a rhyme are cowardice.

Rhyme-first inversion is a tell: speech order first, then hang the chime off the true last stress.

## Pocket / mouth

Write to a grid the jaw can keep without a metronome speech.

Boom-bap: traffic in the middle of the bar. Do not starve the pocket and call it taste. Trap: leave air toward the backbeat; filling every sixteenth is panic. Drill: staccato, few open vowels hanging past the kick. Conscious: still oral. Subordinate-clause essays die on the tongue.

Use the phrase-density lanes in [../economy.md](../economy.md). For airy contemporary trap, start sparse: roughly 3–7 words per verse phrase and 2–6 per hook phrase, with exceptions for a natural anchor or deliberate run. These are English drafting aids, not genre limits. Melodic rap can be balanced; dense bars belong when requested. Count syllables and stress as well as words.

Do not shorten solely because the beat has a high BPM label. Trap often uses a half-time vocal feel. When instrumental space is the aim, reduce the verbal load and leave gaps instead of adding more short lines or automatic ad-libs. Without audio or a phrase map, the fit remains provisional.

A line that needs a gloss after the bar already failed.

Read it at performance speed. If you gasp mid-thought in trap, you overwrote. If boom-bap feels like a caption, you underwrote.

## Flow parity

Verse syllable dial **off**: flow is the design. Bar parity is not optional. Inside one flow pattern, syllables per bar stay in a band and the rhyme lands on the same beat; change pattern only at a seam (every four bars, the hook, a new camera) and name the switch in CAST. Drill couplets: the end-hit sits on the same beat with the same clipped consonant family.

Hook **strict**. A chant that gains a syllable on return is not a chant. Melodic rap hooks match the held vowel on every return.

Trap: a bar that doubles its load because the punchline needed room. Cut the setup instead.

## POV & voice

First person default. Second person is threat, flex, or address — never customer-service “you.” Third person for reportage and story. “We” only when crew is real in the brief.

Economics this week beat brand myth. Named rooms, people-types, bills, routes. Moral residue after the flex. Humor is a weapon, not a relief track.

Words this person would never say are a hard ban. Fake slang salad is a costume and a slur against the ear.

## Hook

One sticky claim or image. Chant beats clever. If the room cannot yell it, it is a stanza pretending.

- Drill — threat-mantra. Short. Repeatable without rotting in eight cycles.
- Trap — a compact desire, accusation, image, or contradiction you can chant or sing back. A shout noun is one option, not a requirement.
- Boom-bap — the turn you wait for, said plain enough to stamp.
- Conscious — thesis a person could argue at a table, not a conference.

For hook-led trap, choose the hook before the verses. Try a stable anchor, a short response or contrast, then an anchor return. Keep the core recognizable across hooks; let the verse move the relationship, power, or stakes around it. Use `[Hook]` if that is the requested label. A user theme is not necessarily locked wording; an explicitly fixed line is.

Do not explain the metaphor in the next bar. Do not TED-talk a chorus over street diction.

## Kill list

- Unearned crown, throne, kingdom
- Bottom-to-top poster with no ledger
- Empty city and brand drops
- “They don’t want me to win” without a they
- “Real recognize real”
- Therapy-rap: wound named, scene absent
- Motivational-poster hook in drill/trap language
- Shadow / dreams / fire as flex
- Identical V1/V2 maps, noun-swapped
- Latinate essay verbs in a street mouth (*utilize*, *destiny*, *transcend*)
- Slang from three regions in one verse

## AI failure modes

Symmetrical struggle → grind → success graduation. Perfect parallel verses. Yoda syntax for the rhyme. No leftover mess — every moral tied. Caption ethics. Cost-free flex (money with no awkward logistics, pain with no bill). Abstract stacks where a receipt should be. Homogenized “lyrical” voice that could be any narrator in any city.

If the verse could be a bio blurb, burn it.

## Briefing questions

1. Who is the narrator economically *this week* — not the myth?
2. What must still be unresolved at the end of the first verse?
3. Which words would this person never say?

## Hybrid note

This file stays primary whenever bars are the product. Steal catchability or melody-air from pop, R&B, or electronic; do not steal their low rhyme pressure as an excuse for empty ends. Run the secondary kill list so the rap does not melt into glitter thesis, Pinterest caress, or a house mantra with extra clauses. Country-trap and similar: keep this mouth’s grid; obey the other file’s proper-noun and dialect ethics. Never costume two slangs at once.
