# Country

A verdict you could say over a fence or a bar top. Place is a character. Work, weather, money, kin. The bill is in the song.

## Sub-lanes

- **Country** — verse-heavy story, chorus as proverb, place-name, or relationship ruling. Stress matches the zip code’s mouth.
- **Americana** — detail-dense verses, plainer chorus, less costume, more ledger. Bridge optional; a last-verse twist usually beats a generic one.

Folk diary without the town economy belongs in `folk.md`. If the throat is a pit, you wanted `punk.md`.

## Form

Verses carry narrative freight. Chorus repeats a ruling, a place, or a twisted proverb — not a therapy slogan. Strophic shapes can work; do not force a pop pre-chorus unless the brief is radio-country and the pre has a lift job.

Bridge is optional. A final verse that changes what we know is worth more than a middle-eight of wisdom.

V2 is a later hour, a different kitchen, a shifted debt — not V1 in a new shirt.

Do not resolve every thread into a greeting-card lesson. Songs in this lane can leave the porch light on and the door unopened.

## Rhyme pressure

Medium-high on the chorus. Verses tolerate narrative half-rhyme. Forced clever rhyme that breaks the dialect is fatal — you can hear the city intern arriving.

Do not write Standard English under a hat and then drop *ain’t* once as garnish. Register is a contract for the whole page.

End words should be sayable over noise. Fancy inversions to catch a chime sound like theatre in the wrong room.

## Pocket / mouth

Singable on an acoustic skeleton even if the record will not be one. Do not pack Broadway density unless you are deliberately in story-song showoff and the story pays.

Stress patterns follow how a person from that county talks. Wrong emphasis is amateur even when the rhyme is true.

Leave air for a steel line, a pause, a swallow. Not every bar is a paragraph.

No fake twang spellings as costume. *Gonna* is speech. *Gonnaaa* and *ol’* wallpaper is minstrel-adjacent laziness. Write words; let the singer lean.

## POV & voice

First-person witness. Second-person letter. Third-person small-town camera. Place acts. Jobs exist. Kinship has obligations.

The town’s economy is not flavor — it is physics. What gets left unsaid at the dinner table is often the real chorus.

Humor under trouble is allowed and often is the adult register. Cheap dead-dog tears without specific grief-behavior are banned unless the brief owns that animal as a character.

## Hook

Title that could be said out loud to a person, not posted as a caption. Proverb with a twist, a place, a verdict.

Choruses in this lane may tell, because verses showed. They may not lecture.

If the hook needs a list of signifiers to prove it is country, it is not.

## Kill list

- Truck / beer / dirt-road bingo with no ownership
- “Small town dreams” with no town
- Dead dog for rent-a-tear
- Fake twang phonetics
- Boots, whiskey, sunset with no scene
- Therapy-speak in a country mouth
- Greeting-card last-chorus moral
- “Roots” and “home” without an address
- Unearned outlaw sticker
- Catalog of gear and weather as personality

## AI failure modes

Signifier pile instead of a life. Perfect school English with a cowboy hat emoji implied. Moral neatness by the last refrain. No county-level detail. Narrator with no job. Every proper noun a famous city, never a local one. Symmetrical healing. Abstract “country” as aesthetic, not as a set of bills and kin.

If you could move the lyric to any state by changing one place-name, it is not done.

## Briefing questions

1. What is the town’s economy and the narrator’s job?
2. What gets left unsaid at the dinner table?
3. Which proper noun must appear — and why does it weigh?

## Hybrid note

Primary when place, work, and proverb-hook outrank diary shame or bar density. Country-rap: this file owns names, jobs, and dialect ethics; `hip-hop.md` owns verse grid if bars are the product — still one primary. Country-pop: keep the fence-post title test; run `pop.md` kill list so you do not own-the-night in a pickup. Americana bleeding into folk: if the shame is more kitchen-diary than county-ledger, switch to `folk.md`. Never stack costume country and costume trap slang in one mouth.
