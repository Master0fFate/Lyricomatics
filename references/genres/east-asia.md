# East Asia

Idol concept as bible. English as a portion, not a diary dump. Phonetic ease for non-native singers. Do not invent Korean or Japanese lines without competence.

## Sub-lanes

- **K-pop (English portion)** — highly sectional: verse, pre, chorus, post, dance-break chops, optional rap lane, bridge, double chorus. English hooks common. Title as choreography accent.
- **J-pop (English portion)** — often tighter native prosody off-page; English is selective. Keep English simple-smart. Do not American-songwriter the whole track.

Full-native drafts are out of scope unless a competent speaker is on the brief. If they ask you to generate native lines you cannot stand behind, refuse that part and write the English portion plus a CAST note.

## Form

Sectional contrast is the craft. Soft verse / hard chorus is a classic; not the only one. Post-chorus may be a title stump, a chant, a fragment. Rap section is a color if the brief assigns it — write that lane in a different density, not as a second chorus.

Do not collapse into a three-part American radio shape if the brief wants the full obstacle course. Also do not write unique dense lyrics for every section; some sections are stamps.

Member-specific lines only if the brief allows multiple POV colors. Otherwise one addressee, clean.

Bridge should tilt concept, not restate “shine.” Final double chorus may add one twist line, not a new essay.

## Rhyme pressure

English sections: pop-clean hooks. Verses can breathe. Do not over-rhyme as if proving English fluency.

Do not over-scheme lines that will sit next to native bars you are not writing — leave syllable budget and air so a later native pass can live.

Identical title hits can be branding. Title in every line of every section is fear.

No inverted “break the silence of my soul” syntax. If a native English speaker would not say it under pressure, cut it.

## Pocket / mouth

English hooks must be globally chantable and easy in non-native phonology. Avoid dense clusters: *sixths*, *strengths*, stacked inner consonants, tongue-twister idioms.

Open vowels on choreography holds. Short words on gesture hits. Post-chorus fragments should be easy to punch.

Idioms that require cultural double-take usually fail on first listen and fail in the mouth. Simple-smart beats broken-grand.

If a line is for a dance break, it must survive being chopped. Paragraphs will not.

## POV & voice

Idol address to fan (“you”), self-empowerment, crush, or cinematic concept persona. Multiple members can mean multiple colors — only with a brief.

Concept bible is law: dark, school, futuristic, coy, martial, etc. Images must be faithful. Export-pop sky/fire/dream packs are how concepts die.

Do not write a full American singer-songwriter diary for an idol brief. The “I” is often a persona in a frame, not a kitchen confession. If they wanted folk shame, they wanted `folk.md`.

Keep English un-idiomatic in the good sense: clear, current, not slang-dated, not essay.

## Hook

Title English word or phrase that doubles as a choreography accent. Meaning clear at first listen. Easy to yell in a crowd that is not reading.

Post-chorus can be the hook’s skeleton. Do not compete with it by writing a second thesis.

If the title needs a footnote, it is wrong for this pocket.

## Kill list

- Interchangeable “we light up the sky” export-pop
- Empty destiny / shine / dream / fire / heart / night pack
- English no native speaker would say under pressure
- Unsingable consonant clusters on gesture moments
- Ignoring the concept bible
- Full diary-songwriter dump on an idol frame
- Too many idioms
- Invented Korean or Japanese from vibes
- Every section the same density
- Rap lane that is just the chorus in more words

## AI failure modes

Global-market cliché pile. Ignoring non-native phonology. Broken-grand English (*eternity in my soul*). Concept blindness. American radio emotional graduation. Overwritten verses. Fake native garnish. Member lines that all share one witty intern voice. Title stuffing. A hook that cannot be danced.

If you could drop this chorus onto any group in any concept, it is not done.

## Briefing questions

1. Full English, hook-English only, or bilingual split — and who is competent for non-English lines?
2. What is the concept in one sentence?
3. Any member vocal color or rap lane to assign?
