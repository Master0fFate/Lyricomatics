# Latin

Name the lane. Name the language of record. Club heat, romantic ache, or narrative chronicle are different jobs. Decorative Spanish in an English song is not a lane — it is a failure.

## Sub-lanes

- **Reggaeton / urbano** — dembow-aware verse, earworm coro/pre, perreo energy or romantic urbano. Rhythmic syllable play, stutter hooks. Direct you.
- **Regional Mexican (corrido and kin)** — storytelling, pride, romance, chronicle. Names, places, events. Form seriousness. Not a party template wearing a hat.
- **Salsa / bachata** — dance-floor romance, ache, swagger. Coro/montuno thinking: repeats that intensify, not new paragraphs. Bachata leans long vowels; salsa coro wants shoutable turns.

Do not flatten these into one “latino heat” voice. If the brief cannot pick a lane, ask and stop.

## Form

Reggaeton: verse plus earworm chorus, often a pre that lifts into the coro. Energy is body-forward. V2 should shift the dare, the hour, or the power — not reprint the same heat with a new pet name.

Corrido / narrative regional: facts in motion. Event, name, place, consequence. Do not force a club pre-chorus onto a chronicle. Respect the tradition’s seriousness; this is not costume crime-romance unless the brief owns that ethics conversation.

Salsa/bachata: sections serve coro repeats. Write a coro the plaza or floor can scream; verses prove the ache or the swagger. Montuno-like repetition is architecture.

Language of record decides structure more than English-pop habit. Do not default to verse–pre–chorus–bridge because that is what you write on Tuesdays.

## Rhyme pressure

Spanish-language traditions often want more consistent vowel-rhyme / assonance than English pop. If the page is Spanish or Spanglish, do not force English multi-rhyme culture onto it.

If the page is English, do not sprinkle rhymes in Spanish for garnish.

Match the language you were actually given. End words should be shoutable in the lane: coro simplicity, corrido clarity, urbano bounce.

No inverted syntax to look poetic. Speech order for the character in front of you.

## Pocket / mouth

Reggaeton: rhythmic syllable play, stutter, chopped hooks. Percussive without turning into a rap audition unless CAST said a rap verse.

Bachata: smoother long vowels, ache that can be held. Do not cluster-chew the title.

Corridos: narrative clarity over ornamental mouth-feel. Names must be sayable and earned with predicates.

Leave coro air. A paragraph coro is an English-pop mistake.

## POV & voice

Direct you is common. Gendered performance follows the artist, not a stereotype pack. Corrido: third-person chronicle or first-person claim — pick.

Code-switch only if true to the brief. A single “mami” in an otherwise Midwest-English pop song is confetti.

Tourist spicy-lover stereotypes are banned. Empty dance commands (*baila conmigo* as the entire personality) are banned.

Narco aesthetic, violence glamor, and crime-romance are ethics-gated. If the brief did not ask, do not cosplay. If it did, still refuse cartoon blood and unearned proper nouns. Community standards outrank your appetite for danger-chic.

Pride and consequence can coexist. Body and vow can coexist. Flattened “heat” cannot.

## Hook

Coro a club or plaza can scream. Titles as nicknames, places, vows, warnings.

Corrido titles often name the person, place, or event. Do not replace that with a generic club thesis.

Simplicity in the coro is craft. Cleverness belongs in the verse if it belongs at all.

## Kill list

- Tourist spicy-lover stereotypes
- Random Spanish for flavor in an English song
- Narco cosplay without understanding or consent in the brief
- Salsa reduced to “baila conmigo”
- Flattening all lanes into one heat-voice
- Writing a serious corrido as party reggaeton
- Spanglish confetti
- Empty dance-command choruses
- English pop structure with a pet name sprinkled
- Unearned crime-romance glam

## AI failure modes

Decorative bilingualism. One “Latino” narrator for three countries and four genres. Club commands without a person. Chronicle without facts. Ethics sleepwalking. Assonance ignored or English multis forced. Coro too wordy to shout. Therapy-pop in translation.

If you cannot say which lane and which primary language, you are not ready to draft.
