# Pop

A feeling you can text. A situation you could costume. The chorus is a thesis the mouth learns on the first pass.

## Sub-lanes

- **Pop** — verse proves, pre withholds, chorus claims. Title as a message you might actually send.
- **Dance-pop** — same skeleton plus a post-chorus that can be empty-catchy. Body in the room. Floor, not diary.

If they want club mantra with almost no verse, that is `electronic.md`. If they want belted confession with run-space, check `rnb.md` before you default here.

## Form

Verse → pre → chorus. Optional post-chorus as brand stamp. Bridge as a tilt — new info, time jump, opposite power — not a fancier chorus.

Radio instinct: land the title early enough to own the song, not in every line like a nervous tic. Pre-chorus lifts by *withholding* the title one bar longer, not by leaking the whole claim.

Each section earns rent. If the pre already says the chorus, cut the pre or starve the chorus until the pre has a job.

Second verse changes camera, clock, or who has the phone. Isomorphic verses with noun-swap are dead weight.

Do not invent a bridge because “songs have bridges.” A post or a break is often the grown choice.

## Rhyme pressure

Medium. Clean ends on the hook. Verses may breathe in half-rhyme and family rhyme. Forced perfect rhyme in verses sounds like a toy.

Identical rhyme (love/love) is weak unless you mean mantra, and mantra may belong in electronic.

End word carries meaning, not a particle you backed into. Do not invert speech to catch a chime — the tell is instant.

Internal rhyme can flavor a verse. It must not turn the verse into a rap audition unless the brief said so.

## Pocket / mouth

Chorus lines are mouth-easy on a tired first listen. Open vowels on the notes that will be held. Consonant clusters kill belted hooks — dump the sixths and strengths and clenched stacks.

Stable syllable count in the chorus; verses may wander a little. If the hook’s word count mutates every pass, it will not stamp.

Speak it without the imaginary track. If you cannot, it is poetry cosplay.

Dance-pop: leave a little air for the body. Do not carpet-bomb the chorus with clauses.

## POV & voice

I/you for romance, taunt, or self-address. Collective “we” only for an anthem that earned a crowd — unearned we is a campaign ad.

Proper nouns, time crumbs, a room, an app, a car interior. Abstract empowerment lists are the enemy. Therapy vocabulary in glitter clothing is still therapy vocabulary.

Diction stays singable and contemporary to the narrator, not essay-adult unless the character is.

## Hook

One emotional thesis plus one image or situation. Claim, plead, or taunt — pick one verb for the chorus and do not smuggle the other two in as a bullet list.

Post-chorus may be nonsense syllables, a truncated title, a gesture. Better empty-catchy than over-written. A post that restates the chorus in new adjectives is a second chorus with no courage.

Title delay can be a lift. Title stuffing is fear.

## Kill list

- “Tonight we own the night”
- Fireworks, stars, and sky as default cosmos
- Dancing till morning with no room, city, or body
- Broken pieces / put me back together
- Chorus as a list of feelings
- Pre that already is the chorus
- Fire / heart / dreams / shadows as the whole bank
- Empowerment slogans with no cost
- Bridge that says the chorus in a nicer coat
- No proper noun, no timestamp, no object

## AI failure modes

Abstract poster diction. Every section the same density. No situation you could costume-design. Therapy arc: wound, healing, wisdom, triumph. Big Latinate feelings on a small mouth. Perfect symmetry between verses. Caption lines that would sit under a stock photo. A title so repeated it goes numb by the second chorus.

If the chorus could be any song in the same key of “night,” it is not a song yet.

## Briefing questions

1. What is the one-sentence text-message version of this song?
2. Where are we — room, city, app, car — and what object is in frame?
3. Does the chorus claim, plead, or taunt?

## Hybrid note

Primary here when the chorus must be a first-listen stamp and the form is radio-sectional. Rap features: keep this form; let `hip-hop.md` own the guest verse’s pressure, and run hip-hop’s kill list on that verse only. Indie-pop: if the hook is a strange residue image and the band throat matters more than the text-message test, `rock.md` is primary. K-pop English hooks may *sound* like this file; load `east-asia.md` anyway for sectional and phonology rules. Dance-pop bleeding into house: if lyric real estate collapses to one loop sentence, switch primary to `electronic.md`.
