# Theatre

A person wants something in this room, now. The song is an action, not a mood bath. Rhyme serves character intelligence — it does not outrank it.

## Sub-lanes

- **I-want / conditional** — need statement; active verbs toward a goal; subtext under the ask.
- **Charm / comic list** — zipper lists that escalate; each item turns; wit matches the character’s class and era, not the lyricist’s.
- **Eleven-o’clock / reprise** — the same title with a new meaning; reprise is an argument, not a copy.

Pop ballad feelings with a “musical” label still fail here. If there is no objective, it is not this pocket.

## Form

Verse as scene. Chorus as need. The song must advance plot or deepen character — preferably both. Information that the scene already stated does not need to be sung unless singing changes it.

Reprise changes meaning. If the reprise is identical in implication, you wasted the device.

Optional dance breaks are production; on the page, keep the spine of need. Do not write a novel between hooks.

Section labels should match theatrical function when useful (`[Verse]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Reprise]`, button). Do not fake a radio pre-chorus unless the show’s idiom is pop-pastiche and CAST says so.

## Rhyme pressure

High craft expected. Clean rhyme, internal play, character-appropriate vocabulary. Perfect rhyme is a tool, not a god. A character who would not hunt a triple should not suddenly become Sondheim-by-default unless that *is* the show’s contract.

Rhyme must not boss word order into unspeakable lines. Prosody disasters (*IN-to* on the wrong beat, function words on the money note) are amateur even when the couplet is clever.

If you have to invert into Yoda to rhyme, you do not have a line yet.

## Pocket / mouth

Prosody is law. Stressed speech syllables on strong beats. Wrong-emphasis is a firing offense.

Character must be able to say it in their intelligence, class, period, and breath. A scullery wit and a prince do not share a thesaurus.

No melisma greed on the page. Write the spine; the singer may bloom.

List songs need escalating stress, not a catalog dump. The last item should recast the list.

## POV & voice

Character voice only. Not lyricist essay. Not a homogenized “witty musical” register for every role.

Direct address to another character, to the audience as a device, or to the self as a tactic. Name who else is onstage. Songs pretend-private while someone listens are a classic engine — use it or know why not.

“I feel” without “I do / need / plan” is a pop ballad in a costume. Feeling may color; objective drives.

Period and class diction are research from the brief, not garnish. Do not modernize a period mouth with app slang unless the book is anachronistic on purpose.

## Hook

The need statement. A title that can reprise ironically later.

The hook should still work if spoken as an ask or a vow in the scene. If it only works as a pop chorus, you are in the wrong pocket or the wrong moment.

Subtext: they can lie. The music of the line should let the actor play the lie.

## Kill list

- Generic “follow the dream”
- Emotion without an objective
- Rhyme showing off past the character’s intelligence
- Rhyme-bossed word order
- “I feel” with no “I do/need/plan”
- All characters in one witty register
- Plot summary instead of dramatized need
- Pop-ballad middle with theatre labels
- Reprise that means the same thing
- Homogenized diction across class/age

## AI failure modes

Everyone sounds like the same clever writer. Songs that recap the book. Inversions for rhyme. No one else onstage. Want-songs with no want. Charm songs that list without escalating. Anachronistic slang. Sentiment bridge. Title stuffing. Characters who announce their archetypes.

If you could give the number to a different character without rewriting diction, the character is not on the page.

## Briefing questions

1. What does the character want *in this room right now*?
2. Who else is onstage, and what must they not be told plainly?
3. Intelligence, class, and period of the speech?

## Hybrid note

Primary whenever objective-in-the-room is the engine. Pop musical: stay here for character and prosody; run `pop.md` kill list so the chorus is not an empowerment bullet list. Jazz-age pastiche: steal `jazz.md` scansion tyranny; keep this file’s objective. Folk/story-song in a show: `folk.md` only if it is a concert moment without scene-want; otherwise stay. Do not hybrid your way out of who is onstage.
