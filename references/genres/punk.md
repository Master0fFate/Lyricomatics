# Punk

A slogan you could paint on a wall. Indictment with a named target. One-breath fists. If it needs a footnote or a pretty hold, it is not this throat.

## Sub-lanes

- **Punk** — short, repeatable, slant-rhyme shout, chorus as slogan, verses as charges. Bridges optional and often unnecessary.
- **Hardcore** — still shorter patience. Breath-group equals one fist. No melisma, no tongue-twisters, no soft-pop vowels hanging around. Target of indictment must be sayable at full throat.

Rock with a leather jacket is `rock.md`. Diary shame with a sneer but no enemy is `folk.md` in a bad mood.

## Form

Short. Repeatable. Verse–chorus if you even bother. Anti-chorus and one-line mantras are native. A second verse must add a charge, a name, or a worse example — not a photocopy with new adjectives.

Bridges are optional. A break that repeats the slogan harder is more in-lane than a middle eight of analysis.

Do not write verses longer than the patience of a pit. If you are still explaining by line eight, you are lecturing, and lectures get bottled.

Hardcore: fewer unique lines, more impact per line. Repetition is a weapon.

## Rhyme pressure

Low. Slant and shout-match matter more than scheme. Perfect cute rhyme can sound like a joke you did not mean — unless the joke is the point.

End-hits can slam on the same consonant family. Do not invert to catch a pretty chime. Speech order, barked.

Internal rhyme is optional percussion, not a rap audition, unless CAST named that hybrid.

## Pocket / mouth

Shoutable in one-breath chunks. Hard consonants. Short clauses. No tongue-twisters. No clusters that trip a full-throat yell.

Chorus = slogan. If it cannot be painted, it is too long or too clever.

Open vowels only when they help the shout (*no, go, you, out, down*). Perfume vowels on a hardcore brief are a category error.

Melismatic soft-pop lines are banned. If you wrote a hold that wants a run, you wandered into `rnb.md` or `pop.md` without permission.

Read it as if you have no air left. Then cut 20%.

## POV & voice

Alienation first person, accusatory second, collective we/they. “They” needs a noun: boss, landlord, board, algorithm, cop, pastor, brand — something in the world.

Generic rebellion (“against the system”) is karaoke. Name the rule.

Humor can be acid. Self-pity without a target is emo tourism. Preachy left-leaflet diction that no one would spit is another kind of costume.

Do not thesaurus the rage. Small verbs: pay, wait, cut, kick, owe, lie, lock.

People-types are allowed; fake proper-noun dumps are not. Earn a name with a predicate.

## Hook

Slogan. Wall, shirt, chant, last-call bark. Same sentence the second time, meaner.

Verses indict. Chorus stamps. Do not let the chorus become a TED talk with distortion implied.

If the hook could also be a corporate “be yourself” ad, it failed.

## Kill list

- Rebellion with no target
- Pit-length verses of explanation
- Thesaurus emo
- Melismatic soft-pop lines on a hardcore brief
- “The system” without a system
- Lecture instead of spit
- Broken-glass furniture as personality
- Identical rise-up choruses
- Pretty inversions for rhyme
- Diary therapy in a spike jacket

## AI failure modes

Generic teenage doom. Slogan without a noun. Essay punk. Soft vowels. Every verse a paragraph. Moral sticker ending. Interchangeable “we will not be silenced.” No job, no rent, no rule, no name. Rock-chorus padding. Hardcore that is actually pop-punk cute without admitting it.

If a cop, a landlord, or a manager cannot tell they were meant, you did not aim.

## Briefing questions

1. Punk shout or hardcore throat — how long is one breath-group?
2. Who or what is the enemy, named?
3. Is the chorus a wall-slogan, a taunt, or a we-chant?

## Hybrid note

Primary when one-breath slogan and named indictment lead. Pop-punk: if cute belted radio hook leads, `pop.md` is primary and this kill list still murders generic rebellion; if the chorus must be yelled as a wall-slogan, stay here and steal pop’s title discipline only. Post-punk / indie: `rock.md` if unexplained image and band throat lead; stay here if spit and target lead. Rap-punk: `hip-hop.md` if bars are the product; this mouth’s breath-group and enemy rule still cut the lecture. Metal-hardcore: `metal.md` if doctrine/metaphor domain leads; this file if the living enemy and fist-breath lead. Never hybrid into a pretty hold.
