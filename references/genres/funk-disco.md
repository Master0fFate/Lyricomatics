# Funk and Disco

The groove is the argument. The lyric is a call, a stamp, a dare, and the bass takes every syllable the voice leaves behind.

## Sub-lanes

- **Funk** — one- or two-chord vamp, a punctuated percussive vocal, call-and-response between lead and backing. Horn stabs live in the gaps between vocal lines. One-line cues are structure.
- **Disco** — phrases repeated endlessly as the sensual device. The lyric is pared to the minimum that propels the beat. Lush hook, strings and a chant. Longing and release on a floor.
- **Boogie / modern funk-pop** — talk-box flirt, party instruction, a title that is a command.
- **Nu-disco / funk-house** — a sentence repeated over a filter. The chant is the song.

A four-on-the-floor mantra with almost no verse and nobody answering is `electronic.md`. Sung ache with runs is `rnb.md`. A praise vamp is `gospel.md`.

## Form

**Vamp default:** funk verses 2–4 short phrases, hook or chant 1–2 unique phrases plus a response slot; disco verses 2–4, hook 1–4 with long-form repeats; boogie verses 4–6 with a 2–4 phrase command chorus; nu-disco 0–2 unique phrases. See [../economy.md](../economy.md).

The vamp is the form. Verse sets up the chant, the chant pays it off, the break strips to drums and a cue. Write the call and leave the response slot empty on the page, or write it short. The room fills it.

Disco repeats are architecture. A phrase said sixteen times is one line printed once with a count, not sixteen lines of development. Do not grow it. Pleasure is in the sameness.

V2 changes who walked in, what hour it is, or what the narrator wants now. It is a short new setup the same chant can still pay off. A verse that tells a story the vamp cannot hold is the wrong verse.

No bridge of feelings. The bridge is the break: drums, a stop, a cue line, the chant coming back heavier. Strings or horns may lift it. Words should not.

Invent the song's own cue. Cues like "on the one" exist in this family, and the classics already own theirs.

## Rhyme pressure

Low to medium. In a chant the repeated word is the rhyme. Identical hits are the point.

Funk verses are percussive: short lines, rhyme or no rhyme, whichever lands the hit. Disco hooks may chime on an open vowel; verses stay plain. Boogie can flirt in couplets if the couplet still sounds like talk.

A clause that needs a comma cannot be shouted. Do not bend a sentence into essay grammar to catch a chime.

## Pocket / mouth

The voice is a drum with a vocabulary. Short words, hard consonants on the hit, silence where the bass is speaking. Leave the gap. If the bass line has nothing to do, the lyric is too busy.

Disco: long open vowels on the lift, easy to sing out of breath. Funk: clipped, percussive, punched on the stress. Boogie: talk-box phrases stay short, vowel-heavy, and flirty rather than finished. Nu-disco: one sentence that survives forty cycles.

Dance instructions are legal when a person on a stage would shout them. A fitness app is not that person.

Speak-test: shout the call across a room, then say nothing for the length of the answer. If the silence feels wrong, the call is too long. If the answer cannot be shouted back by someone who heard it once, rewrite it.

## Flow parity

Chant and hook **strict**. Call-and-response pairs **strict** to each other: the answer is the same length every time so the room can land it. Verses **tight**. Shouted pockets match the hit, not the hold, so the shout vowel stays the same on every return.

Disco long-form repeats must be identical on the page. If it is sung twelve times, it is one skeleton twelve times.

Trap: a response line that grows a syllable so it reads as a full sentence. The room answers in shouts, not grammar.

## POV & voice

Funk: first-person strut or second-person address to the room, the band, or one person at the bar. Disco: second-person want, with "we" only when the floor is actually present. Boogie: the flirt, talking to one person.

Period slang lifted from a seventies movie is costume. If the brief is retro, write a present-tense want in a retro room.

Diction is what a person says over a loud room: short, direct, a little rude. Nothing a brochure would print.

## Hook

A command, a question, or a one-line boast. Boogie: a title that is a command. Nu-disco: one sentence.

It survives forty repeats on a floor and a room that answers. It is stampable without a sheet. It carries a person, a place, or a want, not a feeling list.

Do not write a hook the same length as every verse line. The chant is the shortest thing in the song. That is why it works.

## Kill list

- "Get on the floor" with no floor
- "Shake it" with no body
- "Tonight we celebrate" brochure
- Disco as glitter nostalgia with no present-tense want
- A verse that tells a story the vamp cannot hold
- Essay grammar in a shout
- A bridge of feelings
- Every line the same length as the chant
- Dance commands a fitness app would read out
- A cue borrowed from a classic instead of the song's own
- A response line grown into a full sentence

## AI failure modes

Verses that novelize over a vamp. A chant that develops on the third pass. Call-and-response where the answer changes length or the call is already the answer. Mirror-ball, glitter, groove, and funky as the whole vocabulary. Party brochure with no body in the room. Slow R&B ache under a dance label. Hooks that need breath and a comma. Retro costume slang. Every line at one density. Explaining the dance or the feeling the groove already carries.

If the floor cannot answer it on the second pass without the sheet, it is not done.

## Briefing questions

1. Funk vamp, disco floor, boogie flirt, or nu-disco loop?
2. What is the call, and what does the room answer?
3. What is the song's own cue, the one line the band or the floor moves on?

## Hybrid note

Primary when a vamp, a chant, and a response slot lead. `electronic.md` donates its kill list (brochure transcendence, explaining the mantra, a pop novel on a loop). `rnb.md` donates for boogie flirt (Pinterest chorus, advice-nouns). `pop.md` donates when the command hook risks turning into an empowerment list. Disco-house: `electronic.md` primary if one loop sentence and nobody answers; this file if the answer is part of the song. Funk-rap: `hip-hop.md` primary if bars are the product. Gospel-funk: `gospel.md` primary if God is the addressee, with this file's response-slot rule. Never average a four-on-the-floor mantra with a funk vamp: one is a loop, the other is a conversation.
