# Folk

A kitchen, a voicemail, a receipt, a name. The refrain is the thing they keep almost admitting. Pretty is optional. Shame is not abstract.

## Sub-lanes

- **Folk** — strophic or verse-heavy story; place and work may appear, but the camera is closer than country-ledger. Moral ambivalence. Last lines that open.
- **Singer-songwriter** — diaristic I, letter to you, occasional character study. Time elastic. Line break honesty over scheme. Title often understated.

If the town’s economy and a bar-top proverb are the engine, `country.md` is the better primary. If a band throat and a shirt-print chorus dominate, `rock.md`.

## Form

Flexible: verse–chorus, verse–verse–bridge, folk strophic, monologue with a refrain. Do not force a radio pre because the user said “song.”

Time can skip. Information cannot stall. Each verse needs a reason — new hour, new object state, new power — or it is a reprint.

Bridge optional. A last verse that refuses wisdom is often the adult ending.

Do not graduate. Three-act therapy (wound / insight / healing) is the counterfeit of this lane.

Choruses here may be quiet. They still have a job: the admission that keeps almost happening.

## Rhyme pressure

Low to medium. Honesty of the line break beats a scheme that winks. Clever rhyme that congratulates itself breaks intimacy.

Slant is welcome. Identical mantra rhyme is rare here unless the refrain is a real obsession.

Do not invert into poetry-voice to catch a chime. These narrators talk. If they do not talk like that, you are in theatre or jazz.

End on the meaning word. Do not park *of* or *to* on the hold because you got stuck.

## Pocket / mouth

Conversational. Allow ugly stresses if they sound like speech. Do not pretty every line.

Breath groups follow thought, not bar fetish. Still: if you cannot say it to one person in a room, it is not folk diction, it is a caption.

Leave space. Overwritten folk is pop with a chair.

Do not costume-dialect a region you were not given. Plain correct speech beats fake rural phonetics.

## POV & voice

Diaristic I. Letter to you. Sometimes a third-person study that is still the writer’s moral mess.

People have names, or the refusal to name them is the point. Do not replace humans with roles (“the toxic one,” “my journey”).

Embarrassing true detail is the unfair advantage. Without it, you have seasonal weather and a self-help spine.

Who must not hear this song is often the real addressee.

Ending: quieter is allowed. Wisdom is suspect. Poster slogans are kill-on-sight.

## Hook

Refrain as the almost-admission. Title understated — an object, a time, a sentence they would actually say.

Do not chase chantability into a stadium claim unless the brief walked you there. Catch in this lane is recognition, not fist.

If the hook could be needlepoint, cut it.

## Kill list

- “Finding myself”
- Seasonal metaphors with no calendar event
- Tidy epiphany final chorus
- Symmetric three-act therapy
- People replaced by diagnostic roles
- Poster-slogan last line
- “Journey” vocabulary
- Soft-focus healing with no stain
- Abstract “home” without a room
- Prettying every stress until no one is speaking

## AI failure modes

Healing arc on rails. Every verse the same altitude. No embarrassing specific. Journaling nouns. Weather as a feelings app. Narrator who has read about life. Last line that teaches. Identical stanza shapes with swapped furniture. No one who would be hurt by hearing it.

If nothing in the draft would be painful to sing in front of the wrong person, it is not this pocket yet.

## Briefing questions

1. What is the most embarrassing true detail allowed on the page?
2. Who must not hear this song?
3. Does the ending get wisdom, or just quieter?

## Hybrid note

Primary when diary shame, understated refrain, and speech-stress outrank county-ledger or band-chant. Folk/country: if job, town economy, and fence-post title lead, switch to `country.md`. Indie-folk: `rock.md` if the unexplained image and band throat lead; stay here if the kitchen shame leads. Folktronica: this file’s ending-and-detail rules; `electronic.md` kill list so you do not write a novel on a loop or tidy the last line into a sermon. Spoken-word bleed is still this diction; do not suddenly become slam-essay.
