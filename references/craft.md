# Craft

Open for a genre-less rewrite, or when the pocket is known and the lyric still has no spine.

A genre file wins on density, hook job, and never-do. This file still wins on stakes, camera, cost, and image discipline.

CAST already named the camera. CUT has to keep it. If you are about to skip a stage, read [room-work.md](room-work.md) instead of this lecture.

## Theme is a pressure, not a noun

“Heartbreak,” “freedom,” “empowerment” are filing cabinets. The song is a person under a clock: they want a thing they might not get, or already lost, and they are making a case about it *now*.

Stakes = what changes if the ask fails. No consequence, no song. You have a mood board with line breaks.

Ask once, on your side of the glass: what stays true if the narrator is wrong? That leftover is often the real record.

**Good:** The chorus is a verb they might regret — stay, pay, leave, lie, call, lock it.
**Fail:** The chorus is climate. Feelings as weather. No bill, no room, no time of day.

## Verse is a camera. Chorus is a claim.

Verses put a body in a place. The chorus says what that place costs.

Do not flip this unless the pocket is mantra — dance-floor loops, gospel vamp, some drill — where the claim *is* the architecture and any verse is color, not plot.

Wrong: verse files the moral; chorus paints the sink.
Right: verse is the sink at 1:14; chorus is the sentence they will not take back.

```
V1  Your key isn’t on the hook. The porch light is still on.
CH  Don’t come up.
```

A chorus may tell. That is not a sin. It is a sin when the verse already told, so the chorus has nowhere to stand.

Section jobs live in [form.md](form.md). Tell vs show is below, not a religion.

## Title is a brand, not confetti

Land the title where the ear needs a tattoo.

Early enough to remember. Not in every line of pre, chorus, and post.

If the title is a punch, delay it — last line of chorus 1, or the bar after the pre lifts. If the title is a mantra, repeat it, but surround it with consequence, not clones of the same noun.

A verse that hosts the title every eight bars is a refrain form, or it is nervous. Pick one.

Title-stuffing is a BLEED kill. See [anti-slop.md](anti-slop.md).

## Information budget

Each section spends new information or it does not earn tape.

New time. New object-state. New power. New fact. Restatement is dead weight. Cut it or change the camera.

A pre that already says the chorus is a spoiler, not a lift.
A bridge that adjectives the chorus is a sermon with a new header.
A post that only stamps the title can be architecture — dance, gospel, drill — if the pocket asked for a stump. Elsewhere it is padding.

If you cannot say why a section exists in one spoken clause, it does not.

## V2 is not V1 in a different shirt

Verse 2 must move at least one of:

- **Camera** — kitchen to parking lot; close-up to across the street
- **Time** — Tuesday 3 a.m. to payday; before the fight to the week after
- **Power** — who holds the keys, the phone, the lease, the last word

Keep refrain DNA. Change the shot.

V1 she has the read receipt. V2 he has the spare. Same fight, different gravity. Find-replace nouns is isomorphism. Kill it.

## Cost in the win

Use this when the song is selling invulnerability — a commercial for a feeling, triumph with no body in it.

Do **not** use this as a tax on praise, party, joke, clean joy, or lament that already has a wound. Do not invent an empty chair to make a paid-off farm or a sold-out room “feel human.”

When it applies: who left, what dulled, which number will not call back — a small ugly price *already in the brief*.

```
I got the house. I still eat over the sink.
```

Only if the house-flex was the lie. If they wanted the toast with no hangover, write the toast.

## One governing image system

CAST named one. Keep it.

Keys, or the car, or weather-as-work — not keys and ocean and angels.

The object may change state: jacket on the chair → jacket in a box by the door. That is development. A new metaphor domain mid-song is infidelity.

One mixed metaphor will poison a whole chorus. If the image is unclear, sharpen the vehicle. Do not annotate it. Do not write the picture and then explain what it “means.”

Leave one speck uninterpreted. Mystery is a sharp object you refuse to caption. It is not a fog of abstracts.

## When to tell, when to show

Not a commandment. A job split.

- **Verses show** — rooms, bills, timestamps, what a hand did.
- **Choruses may claim** — if the verses already proved it.
- **Mantras tell one thing forever** — do not “develop” a floor hook into a novella.
- **Folk often complicates** rather than graduates.
- **Blues complains beautifully** without a TED ending.
- **Theatre want-songs** may state the objective in plain speech; the scene still needs a room and an obstacle.

If you tell in the verse, the chorus is unemployed.
If you only show in the chorus, the hook will not travel.

Ambiguity needs sharp images. Vague words do not create mystery. They create mush.

## Before you call CUT done

- Can you point at the stakes in one spoken sentence?
- Does V2 change camera, clock, or power?
- Is there a price inside any win?
- Still one image system?
- Would a tired person catch the claim on first listen?
