# Lyricomatics evaluation suite

Maintainer artifact. Do not load this during normal lyric work.

Run each case in a fresh session. Judge the output against the brief, the primary genre file, `references/economy.md`, and the Record bar in `references/anti-slop.md`.

## Global fatal failures

Any one fails the case:

- Copies or closely traces a named catalog lyric.
- Violates a stated ban, cultural/language boundary, or critique-only job.
- Claims exact musical fit without melody/scansion data.
- Uses page formatting to fake economy: comma fragments as lines or several phrase slots glued into a row.
- Forces verse–pre–chorus–bridge onto a form that explicitly rejects it.
- Omits a usable memory spine when the form requires one.
- Adds an unearned bridge or over-budget section whose extra lines serve no necessary beat, purposeful recurrence, or explicit user/music constraint.
- Uses a fake dialect or invented non-English garnish.
- Breaks flow parity in a strict-dial section: a chorus return or hook anchor whose syllable count, stress skeleton, or line ending differs from its first statement with no stated reason; or a verse pair that drifts beyond the lane's dial in a lane that did not turn the dial off.

## Scoring

Use the eight 0–2 dimensions in the anti-slop Record bar. Fatal zeros cannot be averaged away. Otherwise pass at 13/16 or better.

Also record:

- provisional sung-phrase count per section
- unique lyric-line count for the whole song
- chosen vocal density and rough word-count range per section; reasons for retained outliers
- syllable/stress or breath concerns that word counts miss
- parity grid for every returning section: sung-syllable count and stress skeleton per paired line, the dial applied, and each pair that breaks it
- supplied BPM versus felt vocal pulse, if relevant; no invented tempo
- exact V2 move
- hook/title position, stable anchor, and repeat placement
- one cut that improved economy
- whether melody-fit claims were properly limited

## Cases

### 1. Current pop, ruthless economy

**Prompt:** Draft a current radio-pop song called “Read at 2:13.” The singer sees a read receipt after ending a relationship, regrets the message, but refuses to send another. Direct, not inspirational. No fire, shadows, healing, or destiny. Just write it.

**Pass:** V1 4–6 phrases; pre 0–4; chorus 2–6; V2 no longer than V1; title or a natural variation lands strongly; same chorus repeats; V2 changes time/evidence/power; no bridge unless it reveals a fact unavailable elsewhere.

### 2. Sparse house mantra

**Prompt:** Write an afterhours house vocal around the phrase “leave the light low.” Adult, cool, no plot, no festival transcendence, no city/neon/rain imagery. Just write it.

**Pass:** Approximately 1–4 unique hook phrases and no more than 0–4 setup phrases; recurrence is the form; open, loopable mouth; no explanatory verse or advice bridge.

### 3. Counted rap verse

**Prompt:** Write one 16-bar boom-bap verse and a 4-bar hook. The narrator works overnight inventory and hides a failed audition from family. Dense internal rhyme, plain speech, no crown/throne/grind/dreams. Mark bars only because I asked for them.

**Pass:** Exactly requested bar structure; a page line consistently maps to each requested bar or the mapping is otherwise explicit; internal motion/punch at least every four bars; hook is simpler than verse; economics and family pressure are specific without prop dumping.

### 4. 1940s-style AABA standard

**Prompt:** Draft an original 32-bar AABA torch standard for a singer pretending not to wait for someone. Dry wit, period-neutral diction, title at the end of each A. No modern app or therapy language. No melody supplied.

**Pass:** AABA, not verse–chorus; four 8-bar sections are treated as a requested formal map while line-to-note fit remains provisional; B turns the thought; final A changes the title’s meaning; no claim of exact scansion.

### 5. Classic blues AAB

**Prompt:** Write three AAB blues stanzas about a landlord fixing the lock but not the heat. Humor allowed. Plain English; no fake dialect, crossroads, devil, or “woke up this morning.”

**Pass:** Each stanza has A, A/lean variation, B payoff; B turns or jokes rather than paraphrases; concrete trouble and wit; no separate pop chorus.

### 6. Narrative folk restraint

**Prompt:** Write a folk story about an adult finding an old school permission slip in a dead parent’s cookbook. The parent’s death is already in the brief; do not invent more tragedy. Quiet ending, no lesson. A refrain may recur. Just write it.

**Pass:** Stanzas 4–8 phrases unless a longer stanza earns internal turns; refrain 1–4 phrases; each stanza changes time, knowledge, or object state; no therapy graduation; ending leaves image/action rather than a moral.

### 7. Theatre objective

**Prompt:** Write a musical-theatre number for a hotel night clerk who must persuade a guest not to open Room 8 before the manager returns. The clerk knows why but cannot say it plainly. Comic anxiety, 1930s setting, no modern slang. No melody supplied.

**Pass:** Character objective drives every section; tactic/obstacle/reveal or status change every 2–4 phrases; diction fits role/era; subtext holds the secret; no generic pop chorus; prosody claims stay provisional.

### 8. Language and cultural boundary

**Prompt:** Write a reggaeton-pop hook in English with “authentic Spanish spice.” I do not speak Spanish and supplied no Spanish text. Make it sound Puerto Rican.

**Pass:** Declines invented Spanish/dialect briefly, offers an English-only original or asks for competent supplied language depending job; does not produce tourist heat, random “mami,” or stereotype; still helps within the safe boundary.

### 9. Critique only

**Prompt:** Critique my lyric only. Do not rewrite it. [Supply an original lyric with a 10-line pop verse, 8-line chorus containing three theses, and a V2 that repeats V1.]

**Pass:** No surprise full song; diagnosis names memory spine, economy, and V2 axis; line/section notes are prioritized; at most 3–5 optional alts; respects user diction; identifies that typography is not the problem.

### 10. Existing melody fit

**Prompt:** Rewrite these four original lines to this stress map: `x / X x / X —`, with the held final vowel on each line. Keep the meaning and rhyme family. [Supply four original lines and syllable/stress data.]

**Pass:** Preserves four phrase slots; matches natural word stress to marked positions; uses singable final vowels; does not add lines; explains any unavoidable semantic tradeoff briefly; fit claim is tied to supplied data, not imaginary audio.

### 11. Clean celebration

**Prompt:** Write a gospel praise vamp for a graduation celebration. The joy is uncomplicated. Choir answers. Do not add grief, sacrifice, an empty chair, or a hard past.

**Pass:** 1–4 unique vamp phrases plus concise call/response; direct addressee is stable; no invented wound or prosperity cliché; repetition invites participation.

### 12. Long form that earns length

**Prompt:** Write a corrido-style English-language chronicle about a union election at a cannery. No crime glamour, no Spanish garnish. Three narrative stanzas and a short refrain. Names and facts are fictional but coherent.

**Pass:** Longer stanzas are allowed; each 2–4 lines advances fact, pressure, or consequence; refrain is short; chronology and names stay coherent; the evaluator does not punish length when motion pays rent.

### 13. Fast pulse, instrumental space

**Prompt:** Write a short current pop-punk lyric at 180 BPM with fast vocal phrases, lots of instrumental space, and no double-time delivery. The singer ducks a call from a boss on a day off. Plain speech, no revolution slogans. Just write it.

**Pass:** Starts with sparse phrases rather than long explanatory sentences; chosen density is visible in the writing; hook anchor returns; no extra lines or ad-lib flood to cancel the space. Does not claim exact timing from BPM alone or split sentences at random to fake short lines.

### 14. Trap half-time equivalence

**Prompt A:** Write contemporary melodic trap about wanting someone who only calls for favors. Beat is 140 BPM, vocals felt half-time at 70. Airy delivery, no bridge, no luxury-brand list. Hook-led; just write it.

**Prompt B:** Same brief, but describe the beat as 70 BPM with double-time hats and the same spacious vocal feel.

**Pass:** Run A and B separately. Both choose comparable sparse/balanced vocal density and leave space. Neither mechanically halves or doubles the word target from the BPM label. Hook is simpler than verse, with a stable returning anchor and concrete relationship behavior. Judge comparable density, not identical lyrics.

### 15. Slow does not mean wordy

**Prompt:** Write a slow 60 BPM R&B hook about refusing to delete a voice message. Long held vowels and lots of silence, very few words. Direct address, no healing language. Just write it.

**Pass:** Honors sparse phrasing and holds rather than filling slow time with more words. Repetition supports recall; no forced verse, no padded minimum, no exact melody-fit claim.

### 16. Deliberately dense fast rap

**Prompt:** Write a dense double-time rap verse and a short chant hook at 150 BPM about a cook trying to leave a shift on time. Busy internals, natural speech, no grind/crown/throne. No exact bar count required. Just write it.

**Pass:** Dense verse remains possible despite high BPM; internal motion and concrete behavior earn the load. Hook has fewer words and a recognizable anchor. Does not advertise guaranteed performance fit or reduce every verse line to a chant.

### 17. Locked hook and useful repetition

**Prompt:** Write contemporary rap/trap about wanting someone who uses your affection for favors. Keep the exact line “I want you but you just use me” in each [Hook]. Repeat that anchor twice within each hook, with a short response between returns. Two lean verses; V2 must change what the narrator does. No bridge, no therapy ending, no brand names. Just write it.

**Pass:** Exact anchor survives, even though its eight words exceed the sparse starting range. Internal hook repetition and full hook returns remain stable; response adds relationship-specific pressure, not explanation. V2 changes behavior without a tidy cure. No title-stuffing correction destroys requested repetition. No claim the text alone guarantees a catchy melody.

### 18. Words are not syllables

**Prompt:** Critique only: why might “Irreplaceable incompatibility” be harder to sing in a short held-note phrase than “You still leave your coat here”? Do not rewrite or invent a melody.

**Pass:** Explains that two long words can carry more syllables and consonant load than six short words; considers stress and the held note. Does not call the two-word phrase automatically sparser or claim exact fit without a map.

### 19. Scoped rewrite preserves an approved hook

**Prompt:** Rewrite only this Verse 1 to make the narrator's behavior more specific. Keep the approved hook exactly as written, including line breaks. Contemporary melodic rap, no new sections, no questions.

```text
[Verse 1]
I wait until you call
I give you all my time
You never stay at all
I tell myself you're mine

[Hook]
You call, I leave the plate
One kiss, another wait
You call, I leave the plate
```

**Pass:** Revises only the verse. Approved hook text and lineation remain unchanged if reprinted. Does not expose or substitute alternate hook cores, add a bridge, or broaden the rewrite.

### 20. Supplied phrase map beats the default budget

**Prompt:** Draft only an eight-phrase pop chorus about waiting for a partner to finish a shift. Keep all eight phrase slots. Slots 1, 3, 5, and 7 must be exactly “I leave the hall light on.” Slots 2, 4, 6, and 8 each get one short response of 3–5 words. No other sections. This is a phrase map, not a supplied melody. Just write it.

**Pass:** Keeps eight phrases despite the normal 2–6-line pop chorus budget; four exact anchor returns survive. Does not demand a new plot fact from every recurrence. Response lengths follow the user map. No claim of exact melody fit from phrase lengths alone.

### 21. Verse parity under a shared tune

**Prompt:** Write a current pop song with two verses, a pre, and a chorus about moving a parent into care and keeping their house keys. No melody supplied. Verse 2 is a month later. Just write it.

**Pass:** Verse 2 line n matches verse 1 line n in sung-syllable count (one syllable of play only at a pickup or tail), stress skeleton, and ending. Pre 2 matches pre 1. All chorus returns share one skeleton. The evaluator counts; a draft that passes density and fails parity fails the case. No claim of exact fit without music.

### 22. Held vowel on the hook return

**Prompt:** Write a three-chorus R&B hook around the phrase "I'm not done waiting." Long held note on the last word of the first line. Change one word in the final chorus to pay the song off. Just write it.

**Pass:** The anchor returns identical in choruses 1 and 2. The final-chorus change happens inside the same skeleton and keeps the held slot's vowel family open. Does not vary every chorus to prove range. Does not add a line to carry the change.

### 23. Bar parity with a deliberate switch

**Prompt:** Write a 16-bar UK drill verse and a 4-bar chant hook about a cousin who keeps borrowing the car. Switch flow once, at bar 9, and say so. No Chicago slang. If you do not have the London register, write plain and flag it.

**Pass:** Bars 1–8 keep one pattern: syllables per bar in a narrow band, end-hit on the same beat. Bars 9–16 keep a second pattern as consistently. The switch is named, not accidental. The hook returns identical. No slang salad; register either owned or flagged.

### 24. Dial off where the form has no partner

**Prompt:** Write a through-composed musical-theatre scene for a dispatcher talking a stranded driver through a storm. No chorus. One phrase the dispatcher says twice, once early, once at the end. 1990s setting. No melody supplied.

**Pass:** The agent does not force syllable parity between passages that never share music, and does not flag the lack of it. The repeated phrase returns with the same skeleton. Stress correctness still holds throughout. Does not invent a chorus to have something to pair.

### 25. Strict dial, full vowel parity

**Prompt:** Write a preschool counting song to five, each verse adding one animal at the market. Four-beat lines, one verse per number, a refrain the kids shout. Just write it.

**Pass:** Every verse is verse 1's exact skeleton with the new number and animal in the same slots; pickups only if every line has one; the stressed vowels keep their family across verses; the refrain never changes. No baby-talk spellings, no moral. Does not borrow a traditional rhyme's skeleton.

### 26. Counted meter in another tradition

**Prompt:** Write a four-stanza English-language corrido about a bus driver who refused to leave a flooded route. Classic corrido shape: an opening that gives place and date, a farewell stanza, eight-syllable lines. No Spanish garnish. Just write it.

**Pass:** Every line counts eight sung syllables in English with the stress skeleton shared stanza to stanza; the opening and despedida are present; no invented Spanish; no crime glamour. The agent names the count as English counting, not Spanish rules, and does not claim the result is a Spanish-language corrido.

### 27. Critique catches drift the user cannot hear

**Prompt:** Critique only, no rewrite. [Supply an original lyric where V1 lines run 8-8-8-8 syllables and V2 lines run 8-11-8-10, with one chorus return that adds the word "still".] Why does my singer say verse 2 feels rushed?

**Pass:** Names the parity failure with counts per line, identifies the added word in the chorus return, and explains the cram in plain words. Does not rewrite. Does not blame tempo or the singer. Offers at most 3–5 alt lines that fix the count without inverting syntax.

## Observed regression (live two-brief check)

A before/after run on the same alternative-rock and sparse-house briefs found leaner verses in the new version. It also exposed a miss: the new rock chorus had 8 displayed phrases against a 2–6 default, and the sparse-house hook was short without a clearly stronger emotional turn. The skill now requires a section-by-section count and a distinct-hook check before delivery. A retest on the same briefs produced a 4-line rock V1 and 6-line chorus, with a 4-line house hook. The rock chorus now meets its range; the house lyric remains sparse. This is a structural regression check, **not** proof of stronger emotional writing or melody fit. Judge the hooks blind with human listeners before making a general quality claim.

## Regression targets

After any change to SKILL.md, economy, form, delivery, anti-slop, rhyme-prosody, flow, or a genre file, rerun at least cases 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, and 10. For phrase-density, hook, or repetition changes, also run cases 13–20 (both versions of 14). For parity or dial changes, also run cases 21–27. Record which checks actually ran; a source review is not a fresh-session generation run, and structural compliance is not listener-tested catchiness.

The v2.2 parity and genre expansion was source-reviewed only. Cases 21–27 were written with the change and have not been generated in a fresh session. The thirteen new genre files were checked for structure, slug references, and absence of quoted lyrics, not by native speakers of the non-English lanes.

The suite passes only if sparse forms stay sparse **and** long forms remain possible. A system that solves over-writing by making every song four lines has merely discovered a cheaper failure.
